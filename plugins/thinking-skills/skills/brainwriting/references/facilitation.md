# Facilitation — Anti-Anchoring and Quiet-First Rules

The method's whole value is procedural, not attitudinal. A group that says it is "safe and open" still anchors on the first senior voice unless the writing comes first and the speaking comes later.

## Before the session

- **Write the prompt, not the ideas.** A 3–5 sentence brief: problem, audience, constraints, what has been tried. Circulate it ahead of time so people can think, but circulate **no example solutions and no draft list**.
- **Set a quantity target.** "Three ideas per round" or "ten per person" is clearer than "as many as you can". Count raw entries, including fragments.
- **Choose the mode honestly.** 6-3-5 needs six people physically or virtually present for ~30 minutes of writing. Solo and async exist so the method is not abandoned when that shape is unavailable.
- **Protect the room.** Same seniority mix, no observers who comment, phones away for synchronous passes. A sponsor who speaks during generation converts the exercise back into a meeting.
- **Decide who clusters.** Clustering by the facilitator alone is fine; clustering by the most senior person is not, because their grouping becomes an implicit evaluation.

## Briefing script (2 minutes)

1. "Here is the problem, in these words. Write ideas, not positions."
2. "Pass 1: your own ideas, five minutes, no talking."
3. "Pass 2: extend your own list, five minutes."
4. "Then sheets rotate and you add ideas to other people's entries."
5. "Nothing is judged until clustering. No idea is too rough. Write legibly, one per cell."
6. "I will call time. Do not discuss during a round."

Then stop talking. The facilitator writes during the passes too, at a separate sheet, so the room sees the rule applies to everyone.

## Quiet-first rules

- **Facilitator speaks first only to brief, then stays silent.** After that, the least senior or most junior participant speaks first in any discussion, or entries are read anonymously.
- **Read before you speak.** In the first discussion round, every participant silently reads the full set of entries for two minutes. No commentary until the reading time ends.
- **Author anonymity during harvest.** Where entries are attributable, have someone other than the author read each entry aloud. This reduces ownership defence and status signalling.
- **Round-robin, one item per turn.** Each person contributes one idea or observation per turn; no one speaks twice until everyone has spoken once.
- **Questions before reactions.** Ask "what did you mean by #12?" before "that won't work". Clarifying questions are allowed during build, critique is not.
- **Build, don't overwrite.** Physical rule: additions in a different colour; digital rule: prefix `build on #⟨id⟩:`. Deletion and rewording are forbidden until freeze.

## Handling dominant and silent participants

| Situation | Intervention |
|---|---|
| Someone starts explaining an idea aloud during a pass | Point to the timer, say "hold that for the read-out", resume. Do not debate. |
| A senior person's sheet fills with detailed plans | Fine during generation; do not let them present it first during harvest. |
| A quiet participant's entries are strong but unmentioned | Have the facilitator surface them by ID during clustering, without naming the author. |
| Side conversations form | Separate seats, or switch to async. Proximity predicts talking. |
| Someone refuses to write ("I think better out loud") | Offer them the role of recording others' entries verbatim — still writing, still silent. |
| Group stalls at two ideas per person | Switch the stimulus: "write what you would do with unlimited budget, then with zero budget." |

## Anti-anchoring rules

- **Independent first, social second.** Even five minutes of silent writing neutralises much of the seniority and fluency effect.
- **Do not seed the board.** No exemplar ideas, no "here's the kind of thing we mean" on the prompt sheet.
- **Hide early columns in async mode** where the tool supports it; otherwise collect via form and publish all at once.
- **Do not attribute ideas during selection.** Attribute after selection if ownership matters for follow-up; before selection it biases the room toward familiar authors.
- **Do not rank by volume.** The loudest sheet is not the best sheet; cluster by theme, not by author or count.
- **Separate the two questions.** "What could work?" (harvest) and "What should we do?" (decision) belong to different passes, often different sessions.

## Solo mode

1. Use a timer; stop when it rings, mid-sentence if necessary.
2. Pass 1 breadth, pass 2 depth, pass 3 simulated perspectives (customer, sceptic, budget owner, operator). Label the simulated ones.
3. Do not cluster until all three passes are complete; early clustering truncates generation.
4. Treat your own favourite as one entry among many, not the anchor. Give it an ID like everything else.

## AI-assisted mode

- **Human writes first when anchoring matters**, or the model's list becomes the group's frame. Use the model for a second batch after the human pass, not instead of it.
- Ask for **distinct mechanisms, not variants**: "give ten mechanisms by which a clinic could reduce no-shows", not "ten ideas".
- Request **build-style output**: "extend ideas #3 and #7 with one concrete mechanism each."
- Treat model output as **hypotheses**: it cannot observe your constraints, and plausible-sounding entries may be infeasible or unsafe. Label them and verify before promising anything.
- Use the model to transcribe, deduplicate, and cluster; keep naming and selection human-owned where the decision has consequences.
- Do not ask the model to rate ideas before clustering; that reintroduces an evaluator into the generation pass.
- A single agent running brainwriting alone is simulating parallel perspectives, not producing independent human ones. Say so in the output.

## Timing discipline

- **Call time out loud**; keep a visible timer.
- **Never extend a pass** because someone is "almost done". Extension punishes fast writers and rewards perfectionism.
- **Keep build rounds shorter than generation rounds.** Reading others takes time; 5 minutes per rotation is usually right.
- **Cap discussion before clustering.** Ten minutes of "initial reactions" is enough to lose the independence you paid for; move to clustering.

## Common facilitation failures

- Letting the sponsor open with "here's my thinking so far" — one sentence can set the entire frame.
- Skipping pass 2 because the first list "looks good"; depth is where combinations come from.
- Reading entries in author order, which turns clustering into a status display.
- Clustering by department or seniority instead of theme, which buries the cross-cutting ideas.
- Ending at a raw list with no probes; brainwriting without harvest is a pile of sticky notes.
- Running it after a decision is already made, when writing is just documentation of the chosen answer.
