# When NOT to Use Brainwriting — and What to Use Instead

Brainwriting is for **generating many independent ideas in writing, then compounding them**. If you need a decision, a transformation of one known baseline, or a single best answer fast, another method fits better.

## Quick triage

| Your situation | Better fit | Why |
|---|---|---|
| You need decisions among known options | Decision matrix, `options-portfolio` | Brainwriting generates; it does not choose |
| You have one baseline and want targeted variations | `scamper`, `morphological-analysis` | Operators transform an existing design more efficiently than open writing |
| The problem is a vague topic ("improve onboarding") | Scope it, or `question-storming` first | Written ideas on a vague prompt are vague in bulk |
| The bottleneck is missing facts | Research, interviews, instrumentation | More ideas cannot substitute for evidence |
| One or two people need to think out loud and build energy | Facilitated verbal brainstorming, accepting the anchoring cost | Brainwriting deliberately removes the social energy |
| You need a genuinely left-field leap | `lateral-thinking` (provocation / random entry), `analogical-thinking` | Writing passes stay within the writer's existing frames |
| You need visual or spatial concepts | `crazy-eights`, sketching | Text loses the spatial and interface dimension |
| The group is unwilling to write or the problem needs live negotiation | Structured discussion with a strong facilitator | The method's guarantee depends on silent writing happening |
| You already have 100 ideas and no synthesis | `clustering` step, or `concept-blending` | Another divergence pass adds volume, not value |

## Brainwriting vs. verbal brainstorming

| | Verbal brainstorming | Brainwriting |
|---|---|---|
| Turn-taking | Serial, one voice at a time | Parallel, everyone writes at once |
| Dominance | Loud/fluent participants set the frame | Writing equalises airtime |
| Idea count | Moderate; blocked by listening | Higher per unit time; every entry recorded |
| Social energy | High; useful for morale and shared context | Low; silence can feel awkward |
| Synthesis | Happens continuously, often prematurely | Deferred until freeze |
| Best for | Small aligned teams, relationship building, one energetic push | Mixed seniority, quiet members, many ideas, timezone spread |

Use verbal brainstorming when the room is small, safe, and the goal includes alignment. Use brainwriting when the room is unequal, large, or the first idea would otherwise become the frame.

## Brainwriting vs. SCAMPER

- **SCAMPER** is a guided transformation of a concrete baseline: it asks seven specific questions of an existing product or process. It produces 7–30 targeted variations and is efficient when the subject already exists.
- **Brainwriting** is open divergence: no baseline required, no operator, quantity-first. It produces 30–108 raw entries and is efficient when the frame itself is unclear.
- Use **both** in sequence when improving a service: brainwriting first to surface what people actually care about, SCAMPER second to stretch the chosen baseline. Do not use SCAMPER as a substitute for open generation, or brainwriting as a substitute for a structured improvement sweep.

## Brainwriting vs. divergent-thinking

- `divergent-thinking` is a compact single-agent protocol: 10+ options, forced lenses, cluster, converge. It is the right default for one agent or one person on an open problem.
- Brainwriting adds **parallel independent authors** and a **build pass**. Its distinctive output is combination and extension across contributions, not just breadth.
- A single agent can run brainwriting's passes alone, but that simulates perspectives rather than producing independent ones. If you only need breadth, use `divergent-thinking`; if you need many distinct batches and cross-author builds, use brainwriting.

## Brainwriting vs. other divergence methods

| Method | Distinctive output | When preferred |
|---|---|---|
| `morphological-analysis` | Systematic combinations of parameters | The problem decomposes into dimensions with discrete options |
| `forced-connections` | Ideas from pairing unrelated stimuli | The group is circling one semantic field |
| `analogical-thinking` | Mechanisms imported from another domain | Local frames are exhausted |
| `reverse-brainstorming` | Failure modes and their inversions | The problem is "how do we make this work?" and needs a jolt |
| `crazy-eights` | Eight quick visual sketches | UI, layout, or physical form is the question |
| Brainwriting | Many written entries plus labelled builds | The social dynamics, not the method, are the bottleneck |

## The premature-evaluation trap

Brainwriting run as a front for critique is not brainwriting. Symptoms:

- Entries are phrased as objections ("we can't do X because…").
- Someone starts ranking during the build pass.
- The facilitator summarises after the first pass.
- Only safe ideas get built on, because risky ones attract comments.

**Rule:** no evaluation, ranking, or summarising until the board is frozen. If the group cannot hold that line, split the session: generation in one room (or async), evaluation in another, with different facilitation.

## Handoff triggers

Use `thinking-router` when unsure. Common handoffs:

| Next | Trigger |
|---|---|
| Clustering step (inside this skill) | 30+ raw entries and no synthesis yet |
| `premortem` | 2–3 concepts chosen and commitment is approaching |
| `options-portfolio` | Many good concepts and a need for a balanced set, not one pick |
| `scamper` | One concept is chosen and now needs systematic improvement |
| `lateral-thinking` | Builds keep returning to the same mechanism |
| `analogical-thinking` | A cluster needs a mechanism from outside the domain |
| `six-thinking-hats` | The team must evaluate without collapsing back into generation |
| Pilot / experiment | A concept has a cheap probe; stop ideating and test |

## Anti-patterns

- Running it with a group that will not stay silent; the method's independence guarantee is gone.
- Circulating example ideas "to inspire" — that is seeding the anchor you are trying to avoid.
- Counting entries as progress: 108 variants of one idea is one idea.
- Skipping the build pass, which removes the only step that compounds contributions.
- Facilitating as the senior person and then ranking first.
- Ending at a raw list; without clusters, concepts, and probes, nothing changes.
- Using brainwriting when the real blocker is a decision, a fact, or a conflict that writing cannot resolve.
