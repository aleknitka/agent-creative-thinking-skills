# CATWOE — Customers, Actors, Transformation, Worldview, Owners, Environment

## Components

- **C — Customers:** beneficiaries or victims of the system. Must be observable — name who benefits or is harmed, not "everyone."
- **A — Actors:** those who would do the transformation. Operators, staff, or agents.
- **T — Transformation:** input → output, stated as a verb. "Unactivated account → activated account" or "queue → served case."
- **W — Worldview:** the assumption that makes T meaningful and desirable. Always state as "W = …". Example: "Activation signals intent and predicts retention."
- **O — Owners:** who can stop the system. Decision-makers with authority over resourcing or scope.
- **E — Environment:** external constraints and taken-for-granted elements (law, budget, platform).

## Test

For each CATWOE, ask:

- Is T an observable transformation, not a slogan?
- Is W stated and testable — would someone plausibly hold a different W?
- Could O actually stop the system, and is C someone other than A?

## Root definition

Compose: "A system owned by [O], operated by [A], to transform [T] for [C], under worldview [W], within constraints [E]."

Keep one CATWOE per system; blending transformations into one definition hides the design bet. Record the root definition verbatim alongside the rich picture.

## Probes

Each CATWOE suggests a probe: the smallest test of W (does activation predict retention?) or of T's feasibility (can A perform T under E?).
