# Provenance — Attribution, Origin, and Evidence

## Named method and lineage

- **Gary Klein**, "Performing a Project Premortem," *Harvard Business Review*, September 2007. Klein defines the premortem as the hypothetical opposite of a postmortem: after the team is briefed on the plan, the leader declares that the project has failed, team members independently write down every reason they can think of, and each person reads one reason in turn until all are recorded. The project manager then reviews the list for ways to strengthen the plan.
- **Prospective hindsight.** The empirical basis Klein cites is Deborah J. Mitchell (Wharton), J. Edward Russo (Cornell), and Nancy Pennington (University of Colorado), "Back to the Future: Temporal Perspective in the Explanation of Events," *Journal of Behavioral Decision Making* 2(1), 1989. Imagining that an event has already occurred — rather than that it might occur — increased the ability to correctly identify reasons for future outcomes by **30%**.
- **Kahneman lineage.** The method sits in the lineage of Daniel Kahneman and Amos Tversky's work on the **planning fallacy** and the **outside view** (Kahneman & Tversky, 1979), and of Kahneman's later advocacy of the premortem as a practical counter to overconfidence and plan optimism. The premortem is a prospective-hindsight debiasing procedure, not a forecasting model.

## What is original here

The following are practical workflow adaptations for this repository, not Klein's text:

- The five-category failure-mode taxonomy (assumption, coordination, incentive, capability, external) and its trigger questions.
- The six-step process (intake, failure scene, independent generation, clustering, likelihood × impact prioritization, hardening with prevention and monitoring).
- The scoring anchors, warning-signal/tripwire format, response types, and tables.
- The facilitation protocol details and the anti-anchoring rules.
- The guided-onboarding worked example and the handoff/comparison tables.

Klein's article is the source of the core frame — declared failure, independent silent generation, round-robin capture, plan revision. The elaborations above should be attributed to this skill as adaptations.

## Evidence and limits

- The 30% figure comes from a controlled study of reason generation. It is a finding about **identifying reasons**, not a guarantee that the resulting plan succeeds, and it should not be generalised to every team or decision.
- The premortem is qualitative. It surfaces plausible mechanisms and makes dissent safe; it does not estimate probabilities reliably, discover unknown unknowns, or replace formal risk assessment, pilots, or base-rate forecasting.
- Facilitation effects are large: a senior sponsor who speaks first, or a missing independent-writing step, can reduce the exercise to a review of already-known concerns.
- The claim that a premortem reduces overcommitment ("damn-the-torpedoes" attitude) is Klein's field observation, not a measured effect size.
- Do not cite the skill's example, failure reasons, or scores as evidence. They are illustrations; treat them as hypotheses to test against your own context.
- Label claims as **fact / assumption / speculation**. A premortem's output is in the latter two categories by construction.
