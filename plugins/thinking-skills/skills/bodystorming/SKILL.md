# Bodystorming

Think with your body before you draw. Bodystorming moves ideation from the whiteboard into the space where the service actually happens, using cheap props and walkthroughs to make hidden gaps observable.

The failure mode it prevents is designing a smooth diagram of a service that fails where bodies meet — at thresholds, handoffs, waiting, and recovery.

## When to use

- The interaction is spatial or service-based and hard to picture from a diagram alone.
- Handoffs, waiting, or recovery paths hide failure modes.
- You need embodied insight before committing to a prototype.

If unsure this is the right method, start with [thinking-router](../thinking-router/SKILL.md) — or see [references/when-not-to-use.md](references/when-not-to-use.md).

## When NOT to use (brief)

- The problem is purely conceptual or backend with no human interaction to enact.
- The team cannot access the space or reasonable props.
- A quick assumption test would decide the question without enactment.

## Process

1. **Intake — define the scene.** State the user, goal, constraints, and the moment to enact (3–5 sentences each for before/during/after). List roles to play. See [references/enactment.md](references/enactment.md).

2. **Set space and props.** Mark the space with tape or furniture; collect cheap props for key objects and boundaries. Keep props suggestive, not realistic. See [references/props-space.md](references/props-space.md) and [scripts/bodystorm.py](scripts/bodystorm.py).

3. **Enact roles and scenarios.** Walk through the interaction at 1× speed, then at varied paces and with edge actors (rushed, confused, low-access). Swap roles halfway. Record video or time-stamped notes. Record the full walkthrough.

4. **Observe friction.** Note where bodies hesitate, collide, wait, improvise, or recover. Capture what was not visible in the diagram: reach, visibility, sequencing, and social awkwardness.

5. **Cluster observations.** Group friction points by mechanism (threshold, handoff, waiting, recovery) and name each as a testable gap.

6. **Harvest probes.** For the top 2–3 gaps, define the smallest spatial or service change, its signal, and the owner. See [references/examples.md](references/examples.md).

## Rules

- Enact at real scale and pace — compressed or imagined walkthroughs hide the gaps they should surface.
- Swap roles — the person who designed the step must experience it as the user and as the staff.
- Props are cheap and suggestive — fidelity comes from bodies, not models.
- Record what happened before interpreting why — observation before insight.
- Label fact / assumption / speculation; enacted friction is observed, not assumed.
- Honest provenance: attribute bodystorming to its HCI/service-design lineage; call this workflow an adaptation. See [references/provenance.md](references/provenance.md).
