# When NOT to Use First-Principles — and What to Use Instead

## Quick triage

| Your situation | Better fit | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Hidden beliefs and inherited constraints block options | `challenge-assumptions` | Inventory-led surfacing; this rebuilds |
| You need variations on a fixed baseline | `scamper` | Narrower; question is what to change |
| You need to break a dominant frame sideways | `lateral-thinking` | Provocations; this is invariant-led |
| You need many ideas quickly | `divergent-thinking` | Quantity-first divergence |
| Facts are missing | **Research first** | Rebuilding on thin ground is speculation |
| Candidates exist and need hardening | `premortem`, `options-portfolio` | Convergence, not ideation |

## Don't confuse

- **First-principles vs. challenge-assumptions:** both question assumptions; this classifies fundamentals vs inherited and rebuilds from the former, the other inventories and tests beliefs.
- **First-principles vs. lateral-thinking:** lateral provokes deliberately wrong statements; this strips to invariants and rebuilds honestly.
- **Starting from fundamentals is not "ignore constraints" — it is source your constraints, keep fundamentals, and add back only what earns its keep.
