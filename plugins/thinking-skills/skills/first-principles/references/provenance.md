# Provenance, Lineage, and Evidence Limits

This skill is a synthesis. Its parts have recognizable ancestors; the six-step process, constraint families, re-add log, and probe format are local adaptations, not canonical formulations.

## Primary lineage

**Aristotle — first principles as epistemic starting points.** In the *Posterior Analytics* and the *Physics*, Aristotle used the idea of *archai*: indemonstrable starting points from which demonstrative knowledge proceeds. The *Physics* inquiry into causes and the four-cause scheme is an early example of explaining a phenomenon by its conditions rather than by analogy. The modern phrase "first principles" inherits this epistemic sense: the basic propositions that cannot be derived from something more basic within a domain.

This lineage matters for honesty: Aristotle's project was explanation and demonstration, not product design. The translation of "reason from first principles" into a design method is a later, practical adaptation.

**René Descartes — decomposition and reconstruction.** *Discourse on Method* (1637), Part II, recommends accepting only what is clearly and distinctly known, dividing each difficulty into as many parts as possible, proceeding from the simplest to the more complex, and reviewing thoroughly. The method of doubt is the engine: strip away what can be doubted, then rebuild on what remains.

The modern skill borrows the decompose-and-rebuild posture, not the quest for certainty. Descartes sought indubitable foundations; first-principles design seeks constraints that are true enough to build on, then tests them. The two aims should not be conflated.

**Elon Musk and contemporary popularization.** Musk's public explanations of SpaceX and Tesla repeatedly frame engineering decisions as reasoning from physics and material costs rather than from analogy with existing products. The best-known example contrasts the market price of battery packs with the cost of their constituent materials, then asks what manufacturing could achieve. This popularized "first principles versus reasoning by analogy" as a business phrase.

Treat the popular version as practitioner folklore, not as a validated method. Public cases are success stories; failures of the same reasoning are rarely narrated. The phrase also predates Musk by millennia — he popularized a framing, he did not invent the idea.

## Adjacent traditions

- **Value engineering (Lawrence Miles, 1940s).** Separates the function a component performs from its current form, then seeks cheaper ways to deliver the function. The "outcome versus solution" move and the attack on inherited cost are close relatives.
- **Axiomatic design (Nam Suh).** Represents design as mapping functional requirements to design parameters while maintaining independence and minimizing information. It is more formal than this skill and assumes requirements are known.
- **Systems engineering and requirements analysis.** Distinguishes stakeholder needs, requirements, constraints, and design solutions. The constraint taxonomy here is a lightweight practitioner version of that discipline.
- **Lean and theory of constraints.** Both ask where the real bottleneck is, but they operate inside an existing process. First principles is willing to replace the process.

## What is local to this skill

- The four fundamental families — physical, regulatory, resource, invariant — and the inherited/preference split are a teaching taxonomy assembled here for coverage. They overlap deliberately and are not mutually exclusive.
- The five classification tests (counterfactual, change, scope, refutation, cost) are a local synthesis of standard critical-reasoning moves.
- The why-ladder stopping rules, the dropped-item log, the re-add log, and the three-alternative rebuild are practitioner scaffolding designed for an AI-assisted conversation.
- "Two or three alternatives" is a working heuristic for contrast, not a proven optimum.
- The probe format is borrowed from product-discovery practice and assumption-based planning; its labels are descriptive.

## Evidence limits

There is no strong body of controlled evidence that first-principles reasoning improves decision quality or innovation outcomes. What exists is indirect:

- **Expertise and fixation research** suggests that experts often reason by analogy to prior cases, which is efficient but can carry forward inappropriate constraints. Structured prompts to reconsider the frame can increase variety, but effects depend on task and instruction quality.
- **Case reports** from engineering and product practice are numerous and survivorship-biased. The method is slow, and its failures are less visible than its successes.
- **Decomposition and functional analysis** have long practitioner support in engineering, but that support is not the same as controlled validation of this particular six-step process.
- **The method can be wrong about its own foundations.** Measurements are conditional, engineering envelopes change, and a "law" can be a rule of thumb. Treat every fundamental as revisable with evidence.

## Practical limits

- It is expensive. For most routine decisions, inherited conventions are cheap coordination devices; rebuilding them wastes time and creates risk.
- It can underweight coordination and institutional constraints that are real even when they are not physical.
- It can produce elegant designs that no organization can adopt, because the binding constraint was political or operational rather than technical.
- It works best where the incumbent is demonstrably inefficient, transplanted from another context, or protected only by "that's how we do it", and where the fundamentals are measurable.

## Attribution rules for use

- Name Aristotle for the epistemic idea of first principles and *archai*.
- Name Descartes for decomposition, doubt, and reconstruction as a method.
- Name Musk for the contemporary popularization of "first principles versus analogy" in engineering and business.
- Call this skill's taxonomy, tests, logs, and process adaptations. Do not attribute them to those authors.
- Do not claim that any of these approaches has been validated as improving outcomes in general. Describe what was observed in the specific case and for the specific conditions.
