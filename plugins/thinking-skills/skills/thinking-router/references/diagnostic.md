# Diagnostic — Intake That Earns a Good Route

Collect only what is missing. Reuse available context, prior messages, and uploaded files before asking anything. Aim for one sentence per field.

## Intake template (copy/paste or turn into a form)

```
Problem (as stated):
Real goal (why it matters):
Hard constraints (must respect):
Soft preferences (nice to have):
What has been tried / what failed:
Who decides / who is affected:
Appetite for unconventional ideas: low | medium | high
Time available now: 5 min | 30 min | 2 hours
Desired artifact: better question | varied ideas | broader perspectives | stronger options
```

- If the user gives a long brief, extract these fields yourself and show your reading back for correction.
- If appetite/time is unstated, infer tentatively (e.g., "sounds like medium + 30 min") and confirm in one line — don't block.

## Diagnostic questions by need

Pick 2-3 at most; they are how you tell which need is primary.

### Better question (reframe) — choose when the problem is vague, overconstrained, or prematurely solution-focused
- Is the stated problem the real problem, or a solution in disguise?
- Who frames the problem this way, and who would frame it differently?
- What must be true for this to even be a problem?
- What would make this problem disappear without solving it?

→ Routes toward: `question-storming`, `challenge-assumptions`, `abstraction-laddering`, `first-principles`, `lateral-thinking`, `inversion`

### More ideas (generate) — choose when you have a starting point and need varied alternatives
- Do you have a baseline concept to stretch, or are you starting from blank?
- How many independent dimensions does a solution have?
- Are ideas clustering around one mechanism?
- Would a deliberate constraint or random prompt help?

→ Routes toward: `scamper`, `morphological-analysis`, `forced-connections`, `analogical-thinking`, `brainwriting`, `crazy-eights`, `constraint-driven-ideation`, `ideal-final-result`, `reverse-brainstorming`

### Broader perspectives (explore) — choose when futures, stakeholders, or side-effects matter
- Whose perspective is missing from the current framing?
- Are you optimizing for one future or several?
- What happens one step (or three) after the first consequence?
- Is this a vision that needs grounding, or a concrete plan that needs widening?

→ Routes toward: `six-thinking-hats`, `dreamer-realist-critic`, `stakeholder-perspectives`, `what-if`, `scenario-planning`, `backcasting`, `futures-wheel`

### Stronger options (turn exploration into decisions) — choose when you have candidates and need to choose or harden them
- How many real candidates do you have, and how were they produced?
- What tradeoff keeps recurring no matter what you try?
- Can you combine the best parts of partial ideas into one?
- If this failed in a year, what would the obituary say?

→ Routes toward: `double-diamond`, `triz-contradictions`, `concept-blending`, `premortem`, `options-portfolio`

## Design principles (apply while diagnosing)

- **Clear trigger:** name when the suggested method fits and one good alternative — don't oversell one.
- **Lightweight intake:** 1-2 clarifying questions max before routing; offer a provisional route you can revise.
- **Separate generation from evaluation:** score first, judge second.
- **Meaningful variety:** shortlist methods that vary the assumption/mechanism/perspective, not just the label.
- **Visible uncertainty:** tag guesses ("assumption:", "speculation:").
- **Honest provenance:** attribute eponymous methods (de Bono — Lateral Thinking / Six Hats; Osborn — SCAMPER roots; Altshuller — TRIZ).
