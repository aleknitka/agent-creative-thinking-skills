# When NOT to Use C-K Theory — and What to Use Instead

## Quick triage

| Your situation | Better fit | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A concrete system needs closed-world mutation | `systematic-inventive-thinking` | SIT assumes a fixed world; C-K expands the thinkable |
| You have a dimensioned design with options to combine | `morphological-analysis` | Morph is combinatorial; C-K is generative |
| You need to vary one assumption and trace the world | `what-if` | What-if is one-variable; C-K grows both C and K |
| You need mechanism transfer from another domain | `analogical-thinking` | Analogical is source-driven; C-K could frame it but is heavier |
| The decision is near commitment and needs hardening | `premortem`, `options-portfolio` | C-K expands before convergence; do not expand after the bet |
| You need quick variation on a baseline | `scamper` | SCAMPER is faster within known knowledge |

## Don't confuse

- **C-K vs. Morphological:** morph's C-tree is restrictive partitions within known K; C-K requires expansive partitions that call new K. If no new knowledge is needed, you are doing morph, not C-K.
- **C-K vs. What-If / Futures Wheel:** what-if traces one mutation; C-K grows both spaces and records the learning agenda. Use what-if for one world, C-K for expanding what worlds are thinkable.
- **C-K as requirement writing:** a requirement is decidable from K ("must send email"). A concept is undecidable — if the starting phrase is decidable, restate it first.

## Handoff triggers

When C-K isn't the right entry point, triage with `thinking-router` (*better question / more ideas / stronger options*).

C-K hands off to:

| Next | Trigger |
|---|---|
| `what-if` | You kept one expansive branch and want to trace its world in depth |
| `morphological-analysis` | C-tree is stable and you want the combinatorial map of its dimensions |
| `analogical-thinking` | A K pocket suggests a far domain to import |
| `premortem` | You selected a branch and need to harden before commitment |
