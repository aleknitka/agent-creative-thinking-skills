# Provenance and Evidence Limits

## Dot voting

- **Lineage.** Participatory budgeting and community planning used sticky-dot
  allocation in the mid-20th century; the technique entered facilitation and
  product practice as "dotmocracy" (Jason Diceman's public guide, early 2000s)
  and "multi-voting" in quality-management circles.
- **Adaptations.** The N/3 budget rule, per-item dot caps, and colour-dot
  encodings are practitioner conventions, not findings from controlled
  research. Different guides use different defaults; state your own.
- **Evidence limit.** There is little experimental literature on dot voting
  specifically. Claims that it "removes bias" are overstated: it reduces
  airtime advantage and makes preferences visible, but it remains a social
  process vulnerable to campaigning and conformity.

## Impact–Effort matrix

- **Lineage.** Two-axis value/effort triage appears in lean and agile
  practice, in project-portfolio management, and in usability prioritization;
  the quick-wins / big-bets quadrant language is common across them. No single
  originator is reliably attributable.
- **Adaptations.** The 1–5 anchors, the high/low split rules, and the
  "money pit" naming are teaching conventions for this skill.
- **Evidence limit.** The matrix structures a conversation; it does not
  estimate impact or effort. Its accuracy is entirely the accuracy of the
  inputs, and it hides interaction effects between items.

## ICE

- **Lineage.** Popularized in growth-hacking practice and commonly attributed
  to Sean Ellis, with variants (RICE adds Reach; PIE uses Potential,
  Importance, Ease). The acronym and the three-factor form circulated widely
  in startup operations writing.
- **Adaptations.** The 1–10 anchors, average-versus-product guidance, bands,
  and the confidence-capped-by-evidence rule are adaptations for this skill.
- **Evidence limit.** ICE is a heuristic, not a validated instrument. It is
  sensitive to scale choice and to confidence inflation; average and product
  formulas can produce different rankings from the same scores. Treat
  differences under one point as noise.

## This skill

The specific six-step sequence, keep/pivot/kill recording, and probe
assignment are an integration of the three methods for group prioritization.
They are designed for transparent, reversible decisions; they are not a
substitute for domain expertise, formal risk assessment, or governance.

Where a score matters, record whether it is data, estimate, or guess. The
provenance of a number is part of the decision record.
