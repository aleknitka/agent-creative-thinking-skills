# Provenance — Attribution, Origin, and Evidence

## Named lineage

- **Synectics** — developed by **William J.J. Gordon and George M. Prince** (1960s, *Synectics: The Development of Creative Capacity*, 1961), formalizing four analogy types (personal, direct, symbolic, fantasy) and the excursion method ("make the familiar strange, make the strange familiar").

## Adaptations in this skill

- The four-analogy walk, excursion timebox, and springboard-to-probe workflow are practical adaptations for workshop and AI-assisted use.
- Call adaptations adaptations when attributing externally.

## Evidence and limits

- Controlled evaluations of Synectics as a standalone protocol are limited and context-dependent; case literature reports that excursions can aid generative divergence when facilitation holds the separation. This skill is presented as a disciplined heuristic for analogical estrangement, not as an empirically validated intervention.
- Tag evidence quality (excursion contents vs. forced fit) and probe results as fact/assumption/speculation.
