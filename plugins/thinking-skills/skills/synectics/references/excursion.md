# Excursion — Departing and Staying Departed

## Purpose

The excursion is a temporary, full departure into the world suggested by the most evocative analogy. Its value comes from staying there — not from solving the original problem while away.

## How to excursion

1. Choose the analogy that produced the strongest image or feeling.
2. Explore that world as if it were the entire problem for 5–10 minutes: what does it look like, what rules govern it, what tension lives there?
3. Record the excursion on a separate page — no forcing back until the timebox ends.

## Rules

- Do not map back during the excursion; premature forcing collapses the method.
- The excursion world must be explored on its own terms; relevance is discovered on return, not during departure.

## Example

If the symbolic analogy is "trusted stranger," excursion into that world: a stranger is trusted at the threshold of a home — what makes trust immediate? Explore that world before forcing it back onto onboarding.
