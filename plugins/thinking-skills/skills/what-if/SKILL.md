# What-If Exploration

One assumption at a time — change it, sketch the world that follows, trace the consequences, and bring back what transfers to reality.

The failure mode it prevents is treating assumptions as background while they quietly bound every idea you consider.

## When to use

- Strategic or blue-sky exploration where "what if X changed?" matters.
- An assumption is load-bearing but has never been varied.
- You want alternative worlds as a source of transferable ideas.

If unsure this is the right method, start with [thinking-router](../thinking-router/SKILL.md) — or see [references/when-not-to-use.md](references/when-not-to-use.md).

## When NOT to use (brief)

- You need stepwise correctness rather than exploration.
- The bottleneck is missing facts → research first, then vary.
- You need multiple futures compared systematically → `scenario-planning` or `futures-wheel`.

## Process

1. **Intake — reuse before you ask.** In one sentence each: the stated problem, the real goal, hard constraints, and which assumption you most want to vary. Reuse available context; ask only what is missing.

2. **Name the assumption to mutate.** Pick one — resource, interface, actor, rule, scale, or time — and state its current form explicitly. See [references/assumption-types.md](references/assumption-types.md).

3. **Branch one world.** Mutate only that assumption and sketch the alternative world in 3–5 sentences. Keep other assumptions fixed for now. See [references/branching.md](references/branching.md).

4. **Trace consequences.** Walk forward 2–3 steps from the mutation: what changes immediately, what adapts, what breaks or becomes newly valuable. See [references/tracing.md](references/tracing.md).

5. **Extract the transferable insight.** Name what the alternative world reveals: the mechanism that matters, the constraint that was hiding, or the opportunity that reappears. State it as a principle with boundary conditions.

6. **Probe back in current reality.** Design one small, reversible probe that tests the insight without requiring the whole world to change. Distinguish fact / assumption / speculation. See [references/examples.md](references/examples.md).

## Rules

- One assumption at a time — never mutate several at once.
- Keep the world sketch short (3–5 sentences) before tracing.
- Separate world-building from transfer — the insight comes after consequences are traced.
- Name the boundary condition where the insight would stop holding.
- Visible uncertainty: label facts, assumptions, and speculation.
- Honest provenance: call adaptations adaptations. See [references/provenance.md](references/provenance.md).
