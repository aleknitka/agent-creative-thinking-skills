# When NOT to Use What-If — and What to Use Instead

What-if exploration is for **mutating an assumption to generate alternative worlds and transferable ideas**. It is not a forecasting method, a planning method, or a hardening method.

## Quick triage

| Your situation | Better fit | Why |
|---|---|---|
| You face several plausible external futures and need robust options | `scenario-planning` | Scenarios are driven by critical uncertainties and plausibility; what-if deliberately mutates one assumption and does not forecast |
| You have a desired future and need the route to it | `backcasting` | Backcasting starts from the goal and reasons backward; what-if starts from a changed present and explores forward |
| You need the ripple map of one specific change or trend | `futures-wheel` | Futures wheel maps consequences of a given change; what-if first invents the change by mutating an assumption. (Tracing here borrows the wheel structure.) |
| You want to find failure causes before committing | `premortem` | Premortem fixes the outcome as failure and asks why; what-if can explore failure worlds but is not an evaluation ritual |
| You only need to find and test hidden assumptions | `challenge-assumptions` | That method inventories and tests assumptions; what-if uses one as a premise for world-building |
| Constraints are clear and you need many options inside them | `divergent-thinking` | Breadth within the current frame; what-if changes the frame itself |
| You want deliberate constraints added to force novelty | `constraint-driven-ideation` | Adds constraints; what-if removes or flips one |
| You want an idealized end state with minimal complexity | `ideal-final-result` (TRIZ) | Focused on a benefit with near-zero added cost; what-if explores arbitrary alternative worlds |
| A single assumption feels arbitrary and you want probing questions | `question-storming` or `abstraction-laddering` | Problem-finding rather than world-building |

## Distinctions that matter

**vs scenario planning.** Scenario planning (Kahn, Wack, Schwartz) builds a small set of plausible, divergent futures around *external* uncertainties, then tests strategies for robustness. Scenarios must be believable enough to guide decisions. What-if worlds are not required to be plausible; their job is to break the assumption's grip and surface transferable mechanisms. If you need a strategy that survives multiple futures, use scenario planning.

**vs backcasting.** Backcasting begins with a desired future and works backward to milestones and near-term actions. It is goal-directed. What-if begins with a mutated assumption and works forward to consequences. A natural sequence is what-if → insight → backcasting when the insight implies a long-term ambition.

**vs futures wheel.** The futures wheel is a consequence-mapping tool applied to a *given* change, event, or trend. This skill's step 4 deliberately borrows the wheel's ring structure, but what-if's distinctive move is upstream: choosing and mutating an assumption to create the world in the first place.

**vs premortem.** A premortem assumes the project has failed and generates causes. It is prospective hindsight with a fixed negative outcome. What-if can include negative worlds, but it does not require failure and does not serve as a risk sign-off.

**vs challenge-assumptions.** Challenge-assumptions is the inventory-and-test step: what are we assuming, which are testable, what evidence exists? What-if assumes the answer and explores the world in which it is false. Use challenge-assumptions to find candidates, what-if to exploit one.

## Handoff triggers

- Insight implies a long-term destination → `backcasting`.
- Insight must survive multiple external futures → `scenario-planning`.
- The mutated change is now real and needs a ripple map → `futures-wheel`.
- Probe needs risk analysis before running → `premortem`.
- Several transferable ideas need selection → `options-portfolio` or `concept-blending`.
- You need to test whether the assumption is even true → `challenge-assumptions`.
- World-building is producing comfortable variations → switch to `lateral-thinking` or `constraint-driven-ideation`.

## Anti-patterns

- **Forecast laundering** — presenting a what-if world as a likely future. It is fiction; label it.
- **Constraint smuggling** — changing an assumption but keeping every downstream constraint, so the world collapses back into the present.
- **World tourism** — generating vivid worlds with no transfer-back step.
- **Premise stacking** — changing three assumptions at once and attributing the result to one.
- **Feasibility policing inside the world** — killing the premise before tracing it. Park objections; filter at step 6.
- **Single-world confidence** — one world is an anecdote. Compare at least two assumption types.
