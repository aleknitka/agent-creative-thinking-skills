# Provenance, Lineage, and Evidence Limits

## What this skill is

A structured workflow for **assumption mutation**: change one load-bearing assumption, build the world it implies, trace consequences, and transfer an insight back to current conditions.

"What-if" is a folk name shared by many practices — counterfactual history, scenario questions, design speculation, and blue-sky brainstorming. This skill does not claim a single canonical inventor or a standardized method. The workflow, taxonomy, and templates here are a repository adaptation assembled from adjacent traditions.

## Lineage

| Source | Contribution used here | Difference |
|---|---|---|
| **Counterfactual reasoning** — David Lewis, *Counterfactuals* (1973); the "what if history had gone differently" genre | The core move: hold most things fixed and vary one condition to ask what follows | Philosophy and history, not a group ideation process; no transfer-back step |
| **Scenario planning** — Herman Kahn and Anthony Wiener, *The Year 2000* (1967); Pierre Wack's Shell work (HBR, 1985); Peter Schwartz, *The Art of the Long View* (1991) | Futures as structured alternatives rather than a single forecast; attention to drivers and implications | Scenarios must be plausible and are used to test strategy robustness; what-if worlds deliberately need not be plausible |
| **Futures wheel** — Jerome C. Glenn (1972); Millennium Project *Futures Research Methodology* | Ring-by-ring consequence mapping (first-, second-, higher-order) | Applied to a given change; this skill borrows the structure in step 4 but first invents the change |
| **Backcasting** — popularized by John B. Robinson (1982; 1990) | The idea that alternative futures can reorganize present priorities | Goal-directed backward reasoning; what-if is forward exploration |
| **Pre-mortem** — Gary Klein, "Performing a Project Premortem" (HBR, 2007) | Prospective imagining of an outcome to surface causes | Fixed failure outcome and evaluative purpose; what-if is generative |
| **Lateral thinking** — Edward de Bono (1967 onward) | Adjacent toolkit for challenging assumptions and provoking alternatives | What-if is narrower: one assumption, one world, one transfer-back; not a general provocation method |
| **TRIZ / ideal final result** — Genrich Altshuller (from 1946) | Idealized states as a way to escape inherited tradeoffs | Focused on a benefit with near-zero added cost; what-if explores arbitrary alternative worlds |

The six-type taxonomy (**resource, interface, actor, rule, scale, time**), the world sketch template, the transfer-back gate, the probe format, and `scripts/what_if.py` are **practical adaptations by this repository**. They are not attributed to any of the sources above.

## Evidence and limits

- There is **no controlled evidence** that this specific one-assumption workflow improves decision quality. Treat it as a heuristic for structured imagination, not a validated method.
- Creativity-training research reports positive but **heterogeneous, facilitator- and design-dependent** effects. Structured techniques tend to help novices more than experts; novelty does not guarantee usefulness.
- Scenario planning's evidence base is mostly **case-based and practitioner-reported**, not experimental. The same caution applies to any claim that alternative-world exploration predicts outcomes.
- Counterfactual thinking can improve learning after outcomes, but it can also produce **hindsight bias** and overly confident "we would have known" narratives. This is one reason step 6 requires a probe rather than a conclusion.
- Worlds generated by this skill — especially by an AI assistant — are **hypotheses and fiction**. Do not cite them as forecasts, user research, or evidence. Label claims as fact / assumption / speculation.
- The examples in `references/examples.md` are invented illustrations on a composite problem. They are not case studies.

## Attribution practice

- When sharing an artifact produced with this skill, keep a short header: *"What-if exploration — method adapted from counterfactual reasoning, scenario planning, and futures-wheel tracing; workflow adapted by this repository."*
- Call the taxonomy, templates, and script **adaptations**, not the originators' methods.
- No third-party text is bundled. All prompts, templates, and script content are original to this repository and intended to be MIT-compatible; replace or extend them only with compatible sources.
- If you later borrow a specific published technique (for example a exact futures-wheel variant), cite the edition and check permissions before bundling long excerpts.
