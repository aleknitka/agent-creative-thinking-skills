# Facilitation — Timing, Space, and Virtual Adaptation

The structure does the work only if the container is held. That means a visible timer, a stated output format, grouping that cannot self-select, and a facilitator who does not contribute content.

## Timing cheatsheet

| Structure | Silent / solo | Pairs | Quads / small groups | Whole group | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1-2-4-All | 1 min | 2 min | 4 min | 5–8 min | 12–15 min |
| Impromptu Networking | — | 2 min per person × 3 rounds | — | 5 min harvest | 15–20 min |
| 15% Solutions | 5 min | — | 10 min in threes | 10 min | 25–30 min |
| TRIZ | 10 min worst-result list | — | 10 min compare (optional) | 20–25 min vote + stop list | 45–60 min |
| Wicked Questions | 5 min | 5 min | 10 min craft (threes/quads) | 15 min test + select | 35–40 min |
| Ecocycle Planning | 5 min read the map | — | 20 min place items | 30–50 min diagnose + decide | 60–90 min |

Rules of thumb:

- Never shorten the silent or pair rounds to give the whole group more time; shorten the harvest instead.
- Put the clock where everyone can see it and announce the remaining time at the halfway point, not every minute.
- Leave 20 percent slack. Structures expand to fill the room.
- If you have less than the minimum time, drop a structure and run one shorter one — do not compress two.
- After any structure, the harvest must be captured within the session. A structure with no artifact is a conversation.

## Room setup

- **Chairs and tables.** Movable chairs, no fixed boardroom table. Pairs need to turn toward each other; quads need a surface. A U-shape wastes pairing time.
- **Wall space.** One long wall for the harvest, one area per quad for working notes. If wall space is short, use flip-chart sheets on the floor.
- **Materials.** Sticky notes, markers for every person, dot votes, masking tape. One marker per person prevents one handwriting from dominating the board.
- **Sound.** For Impromptu Networking, people stand and move. If the room echoes, send pairs to corners; three simultaneous conversations in a small room is the practical limit.
- **Position of the facilitator.** Stand at the edge, not at the front. Do not hold a marker during divergence; hand it to a participant.
- **Hierarchy.** Seat senior people last, or have them write and share in the same rounds as everyone. Do not create a "leadership table".

## Virtual board adaptation

Map each physical affordance to a board feature:

| Physical | Virtual |
|---|---|
| Individual sheet | Private note or a personal column; ask people to draft in a local document if the board is visible |
| Pair exchange | Breakout room of two, 2 minutes each, one shared note per pair |
| Quad synthesis | Breakout room of four, one board frame per quad; each quad fills its own frame |
| Wall harvest | Main board with one frame per quad; facilitator pastes curated items, not full lists |
| Dot vote | Board voting, or a poll with a fixed number of votes per person |
| Silent writing | Disable chat, ask for cameras optional, and hold a full minute before opening breakouts |
| Timer | Shared on-screen timer; announce start and end explicitly |

Practical notes:

- Pre-build the frames and name them ("Quad 1", "Pair 3") before the session. Building canvases live burns the timebox.
- Assign pairs and quads by list, not "find a partner" — in virtual rooms people default to whoever they already know.
- Ask one person per quad to paste the quad's output; the facilitator should not transcribe.
- For Impromptu Networking, use three 4-minute breakout rounds with pre-assigned partners, then a plenary harvest in chat: one line per person.
- Record the harvest board and circulate a frozen image with the decisions, so the artifact survives the meeting link.
- Hybrid rooms: give remote participants their own quad and a named in-room buddy; otherwise the room talks to itself and the screen becomes a broadcast.

## Facilitator moves

- **Open.** Name the structure, the question, the grouping, the time, and the output in under 90 seconds. Say what will happen to the output.
- **During silent work.** Protect silence. Do not fill it. If someone speaks, restate "writing first, then pairs".
- **During pairs.** Circulate; listen for the question being changed. If pairs are solving a different question, stop the room and restate the original.
- **During harvest.** Ask "what is new, contested, or missing?" rather than "who wants to present?" Take dissent first.
- **When time is called.** Stop the activity mid-sentence if needed. "Time. Bring your notes." A timebox that is not enforced teaches the room that the next one is optional.
- **When a senior person speaks first.** Acknowledge the point, then ask them to write it down like everyone else, and continue the round. Do not debate it.
- **When the group tries to fix during divergence.** Park it visibly: "That is a solution — it goes on the solutions sheet after the harvest." Then continue.
- **Close.** Read back the artifact, the decisions, the owners, and the dates. Ask for one dissent or missing voice before closing.

## Common facilitation errors

- Explaining the method's history instead of starting the first round.
- Adding a second question in the same timebox.
- Summarising pair output yourself, which re-centralises the frame.
- Letting quads present sequentially so the first quad sets the standard for the rest.
- Harvesting by theme before reading raw entries, which hides the outlier.
- Running a structure to appear participatory when the decision is already made.
- Ending with energy and no owner, date, or probe.
