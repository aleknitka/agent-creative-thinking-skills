# Provenance — Attribution, Licensing, and Adaptation

## The source

- **Henri Lipmanowicz** and **Keith McCandless** developed Liberating Structures, published as *The Surprising Power of Liberating Structures: Simple Rules to Unleash a Culture of Innovation* (2014). The work grew out of complexity-informed organisation practice and years of facilitated sessions in companies, NGOs, and public institutions.
- The public catalogue at **liberatingstructures.com** documents the full set — commonly described as **33 structures** — with purpose, steps, time, and group-size guidance. The site and the book are the primary sources; this skill summarises six of the structures in its own words.
- The structures share a design stance: **simple rules, distributed control, and no expert in the room**. Each is meant to be run by any participant, not only by a trained facilitator. That is why the descriptions are short and the timing is explicit.

## What this skill covers

Six structures, chosen because they are the ones most useful for agent-assisted and small-team reasoning:

| Structure | Primary use here |
|---|---|
| 1-2-4-All | Equal-voice generation and synthesis in a meeting |
| Impromptu Networking | Connection and shared-context discovery |
| 15% Solutions | Converting resignation into owned action |
| TRIZ (Wreck-the-Org) | Naming and stopping counterproductive practice |
| Wicked Questions | Holding two true commitments in one frame |
| Ecocycle Planning | Portfolio lifecycle, stopping, and renewal |

The remaining structures in the catalogue — for example Nine Whys, What/So What/Now What, Min Specs, Tiny Demons — are out of scope. Do not describe this skill as covering Liberating Structures generally.

## This is an adaptation, not the source

- Wording throughout this skill is **original paraphrase**, not reproduced text from the book or the website. Where a structure has a canonical form, the essential mechanics are preserved and the framing, tables, failure signals, and facilitation notes are this repository's.
- The six-step process in the skill (intake, choose, set up, run, harvest, probe) is an adaptation for occasional facilitators and agent workflows. Lipmanowicz and McCandless present structures as a repertoire, not as this pipeline.
- The **failure signals** per structure are practical observations assembled for this skill. They are not claims from the source material.
- The worked example is invented for illustration. Its numbers, team, and quotes are not data from any real organisation.
- No proprietary worksheets, templates, or licensed materials are bundled. The helper script prints the structure names, times, and steps summarised here; it includes no third-party text or worksheet layout.

## Evidence and limits

- **The evidence base is practice-based, not experimental.** The structures are supported by facilitator experience, case write-ups, and the authors' synthesis of complexity science and organisational learning. There is little controlled research isolating any single structure's effect. Do not present them as evidence-based interventions with measured effect sizes.
- **The mechanisms have independent support.** Equal participation, silent writing before discussion, and structured turn-taking are well supported in the group-decision literature (for example, nominal group technique research on production blocking and dominance). That supports the design logic, not the specific branded structures.
- **Facilitation quality dominates.** The same structure run without a silent minute, with self-selected pairs, or with an unenforced timebox loses its effect. The container, not the name, is the active ingredient.
- **Caveat on TRIZ naming.** The LS structure called TRIZ is inspired by, but distinct from, the engineering Theory of Inventive Problem Solving (Altshuller). LS-TRIZ designs the worst outcome and stops the practices producing it; classical TRIZ resolves technical contradictions with inventive principles. Keep the names separate in engineering contexts.
- **Ecocycle Planning** draws on ecocycle and panarchy ideas (growth, conservation, release, reorganisation) as used in organisational and community development. The mapping here is a facilitation simplification, not an ecological model.
- **Not a substitute for addressing power.** A structure can hold hierarchy at bay for the length of a timebox. It cannot create decision rights, safety, or trust that are absent before the session.
