# Liberating Structures

A small menu of facilitation micro-structures. Each one changes *who speaks, in what grouping, for how long*, and what artifact the group leaves with. The structures are simple to explain and hard to run well — the discipline is the timing and the equal turn.

The failure mode they prevent is **unstructured discussion**: the senior or fastest speaker frames the issue in the first two minutes, the rest of the meeting elaborates that frame, and the people closest to the work never say the thing they came to say.

## When to use

- The group is larger than four and a round-table discussion would leave most people silent.
- Hierarchy, tenure, or fluency is dominating: the manager speaks first and the room converges on their view.
- You need equal voice on a question that affects everyone, not a presentation followed by questions.
- The same issue keeps reopening because the group has never separated generating, filtering, and committing — or has never named what it should stop doing.

If unsure this is the right method, start with [thinking-router](../thinking-router/SKILL.md) — or see [references/when-not-to-use.md](references/when-not-to-use.md).

## When NOT to use (brief)

- The work is analysis one person can do faster alone → do the analysis, then use a structure only to review it.
- A decision is already made and input is theatre → say so; do not run a participation ritual.
- The group is in acute conflict or psychological safety is absent → address the conflict or use a mediated process first; a structure will not contain it.
- You need deep expertise on a narrow technical question → expert review, not equal-voice facilitation.

## Process

1. **Intake — name the challenge and the constraint.** Write the question in one sentence the group can answer, plus the real constraint (time, authority, budget, decision rights). Choose the artifact you need at the end: a ranked list, a stop list, paired tensions, a portfolio map, or named first moves. If the question is vague, scope it before inviting people.

2. **Choose one or two structures for the need.** Match the need, not the enthusiasm: breadth and equal voice → 1-2-4-All; connection and shared context → Impromptu Networking; agency after "nothing can change" → 15% Solutions; repeated counterproductive behaviour → TRIZ; competing truths → Wicked Questions; portfolio lifecycle → Ecocycle Planning. See [references/structures.md](references/structures.md).

3. **Set up timing and space.** Put the minute counts on a visible timer and write the sequence on a flip chart or shared screen. Pre-assign pairs/quads or use counting off. Prepare capture surfaces — one per group, plus one harvest board. For room layout, virtual boards, and minute-by-minute scripts see [references/facilitation.md](references/facilitation.md).

4. **Run the micro-structure exactly.** State the question, the grouping, the time, and the output format; then start the timer. Do not add a second question mid-round. Enforce silent individual time, then pair time, then quad time. Facilitator does not contribute content.

5. **Harvest the differences.** Collect outputs verbatim, then cluster and name themes. Read minority and dissenting entries aloud. Compare groups: where did quads converge, where did they diverge? Divergence is the useful signal — do not average it away. See [references/examples.md](references/examples.md).

6. **Next probe or handoff.** Convert the harvest into at most three moves, each with an owner, a date, and the smallest evidence that would show it working. If the group needs to choose among options, hand off to `options-portfolio` or a decision matrix; if commitment is near, hand off to `premortem`. See [references/when-not-to-use.md](references/when-not-to-use.md).

## Rules

- One structure at a time. Finish, harvest, then decide whether a second structure is warranted — two half-run structures produce nothing.
- Timebox strictly. Call time even when the conversation is interesting; interesting is what unstructured meetings are made of.
- Equal voices: everyone writes before anyone speaks, and the facilitator speaks last on content.
- No fixing during divergence. Do not solve, defend, or clarify while ideas are being generated.
- Use the group's words, verbatim, with stable IDs, so harvest and dissent stay traceable.
- Separate what is true from what is preferred; label assumptions and constraints.
- Announce the structure and its rules once, plainly — no jargon, no lecture on the method's origins.
- Honest provenance: attribute the structures to Lipmanowicz and McCandless; call local adaptations adaptations. See [references/provenance.md](references/provenance.md).
