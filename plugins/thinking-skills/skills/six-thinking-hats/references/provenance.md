# Provenance — Attribution and Evidence

## Named method

- **Six Thinking Hats** — Edward de Bono, *Six Thinking Hats* (1985; later editions 1999, 2017). The hats framework introduced **parallel thinking** (everyone in the same mode at once) to replace adversarial debate. The six colors and their modes — White (information), Red (feelings/intuition), Yellow (benefits), Black (caution), Green (creativity), Blue (process control) — are de Bono's.
- In this repository, the hats are summarized and operationalized for AI-assisted use; no copyrighted text is reproduced beyond short attributed facts.
- The catalog interprets **"Feihatz"** as **Six Thinking Hats** — this is an explicit interpretation noted in `docs/creative-thinking-methodologies.md`.

## Adaptations in this skill

- The sequencing table (quick / exploratory / full evaluation / pre-mortem / retrospective), facilitation moves, synthesis template, timing defaults, and handoff map are **practical workflow adaptations** for this repository. They preserve de Bono's mode semantics but are not his wording.
- The mapping of Green to `lateral-thinking` provocations and the pairing of Yellow before Black as a deliberate order are presented as facilitation heuristics, not as de Bono's prescribed sequence (he describes sequences as flexible).
- Call adaptations adaptations when attributing externally.

## Relation to Lateral Thinking

- **Green Hat is lateral thinking** — the creative mode within the hats uses the same provocation + movement operations defined in that skill. The hats add five other modes; lateral-thinking can be invoked from Green without leaving the hat structure.

## Evidence and limits

- Like other de Bono methods, Six Thinking Hats has not been subjected to large-scale randomized controlled trials; field reports are generally positive but facilitator-dependent. This skill presents the method as a **disciplined heuristic** for separating modes, not as a validated law.
- Do not present Red-hat statements or AI-generated stakeholder views as research — tag them as subjective / hypotheses to validate.
- For sharing externally, cite the method by name and edition and note any adaptation.
