# Provocation Operators — 6 Ways to Make a Po

A **provocation (Po)** is a statement you know is wrong or impossible, used for its *movement value* — where it can take your thinking — not its truth value. Every Po is labelled `Po:` so the boundary between judgment and forward movement stays visible.

> Technique due to Edward de Bono (`The Use of Lateral Thinking` 1967; `Po: Beyond Yes and No` 1968/72; `Serious Creativity` 1992).

## How to use

1. Pick a taken-for-granted element of the dominant idea (identified in the challenge step).
2. Apply an operator below to make it deliberately wrong.
3. Label it `Po:` and **do not judge yet** — movement comes next.

Aim for ≥5 Pos using **≥3 different operators**. If a Po sounds reasonable, make it stranger.

## The 6 operators

### 1. Escape — remove what is taken for granted

**Recipe:** `Po, <subject> has no <usual constraint / component / step>.`

- Po, cars have no wheels.
- Po, onboarding has no tutorial.
- Po, the report has no numbers.

**Why it works:** Forces you to achieve the goal without the crutch you assumed was essential.

### 2. Reversal — flip direction, role, or order

**Recipe:** `Po, <A> does what <B> normally does / <process> runs backwards.`

- Po, the factory is downstream of itself — it must take its water from downstream of its outfall. *(de Bono's classic; later became law in some countries)*
- Po, candidates interview the company, not the other way around.
- Po, customers pay before they see the product.

**Why it works:** Exposes asymmetry and hidden directionality.

### 3. Exaggeration — scale to absurdity

**Recipe:** `Po, <quantity / frequency / size> is 1000× / once a year / near-zero.`

- Po, the telephone rings once a year.
- Po, the team ships every hour.
- Po, the price is 1000× what it is now.

**Why it works:** Breaks anchoring on "normal" scale; reveals what matters at the extremes.

### 4. Distortion — warp time, sequence, or causality

**Recipe:** `Po, <sequence> happens in reverse / at the wrong time / for the wrong reason.`

- Po, effect happens before cause — users succeed before they start.
- Po, feedback arrives before the action that triggers it.
- Po, coffee cups have handles because coffee should not be so hot in the first place. *(de Bono's handle example — distort the causal assumption)*

**Why it works:** Challenges the assumed order you stopped noticing.

### 5. Wishful thinking — state a fantasy

**Recipe:** `Po, <desired outcome> happens with no cost / effort / tradeoff.`

- Po, traffic polices itself.
- Po, forms fill themselves perfectly.
- Po, churn prevents itself.

**Why it works:** Names the ideal without censoring for feasibility; movement then walks it back toward practical.

### 6. Arising (random entry) — force an unrelated stimulus to matter

**Recipe:** `Po, <problem> is like a <random word> — what if it behaved like one?`

- Random word **nose** → photocopier → lavender scent when low on paper. *(de Bono's nose→copier)*
- Random word **harbor** → onboarding → safe shelter where newcomers dock and get oriented by locals.
- Use the offline seeded picker `scripts/random_entry.py` for reproducible random words.

**Why it works:** Introduces a genuinely new entry point, bypassing associative memory.

## Checks before you move on

- Is each Po **deliberately wrong**? (If it could be true today, escalate it.)
- Are the Pos **mechanistically different**, not just reworded?
- Did you use ≥3 operators?
- Are they labelled `Po:`?
