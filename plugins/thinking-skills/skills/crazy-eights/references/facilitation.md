# Facilitation — Solo, AI-Agent, and Team Modes

The procedure is the same in every mode: eight slots, a hard clock, no evaluation until all eight exist. What changes is who generates, how silence is enforced, and how the eight are shared.

## Solo

**Setup (3 minutes):** write the prompt at the top of a page; list constraints beside it; set a visible timer for eight minutes, one minute per slot.

**During:** work in order 1–8. Do not reread earlier slots while generating; do not cross out. If you stall, write a bad version and move on. Typing is fine, but handwriting or a rough sketch reduces the temptation to polish sentences.

**After:** read all eight once without editing. Then run the variety audit. Solo sessions are especially prone to the polite eight — deliberately spend slots 7–8 on an opposite and a constraint-free concept.

**Solo is not a lesser mode.** It removes social smoothing. The main risk is that your own preferred answer occupies slots 1, 4, and 7 in different clothes; the audit catches that.

## AI-agent mode

An agent can generate eight concepts in seconds, which destroys the time pressure that gives the method its bite. Replace the clock with explicit breadth rules:

1. **Fill slots sequentially, one at a time.** Do not draft all eight in one pass and then split them into rows; that produces one concept with eight headings.
2. **Require the mechanism and "differs from" fields for every slot.** If slot n cannot name a distinct mechanism, it is a variation and must be replaced.
3. **Ban evaluation inside generation.** No ranking, feasibility scoring, or "this may be risky" until eight slots are filled.
4. **Keep the human's intake.** Ask for the prompt, audience, success measure, and constraints if they are absent; do not invent them silently.
5. **Show the eight as eight.** Present the filled table, not a polished narrative that hides the breadth.
6. **Timebox the interaction, not the model.** A practical adaptation is one slot per user turn or a strict token/step budget per slot. The purpose is to prevent the model from over-elaborating slot 1.

What the AI mode loses: the embodied sketch, the accidental mark, the idea that appears while drawing. Compensate with more aggressive variety seeds and at least one deliberately strange slot that the model would not choose by default. What it gains: tireless variation and a clean audit trail.

The agent must not present the eight as recommendations. They are material for the human's clustering and choice.

## Team

**Roles:** one facilitator (holds the prompt and constraints), one timekeeper (calls each minute), participants who generate silently.

**Setup:** prompt and constraints on a shared surface; each participant gets eight frames (paper folded into eight, or a shared template with eight labeled areas). Six to eight people is the practical maximum for a readable gallery.

**Generation (8 minutes, silent):** no talking, no showing neighbours, no group sketching. Silence is the mechanism: it prevents anchoring on the first confident voice. The timekeeper announces the start and then only the minute boundaries.

**Share (10–15 minutes):** post all sheets. Gallery walk in silence (2 minutes), then each author states the mechanism of each concept in one sentence — not a pitch. Participants add sticky notes with questions, not verdicts.

**Cluster and vote (15–20 minutes):** cluster by mechanism, name clusters, then dot-vote (each participant three dots, at most one per concept). Keep the raw sheets visible; do not let the vote erase the outliers.

**Remote:** use eight numbered frames per person in a shared board, or eight slides per person. Breakout rooms of four to six for generation if the main room is large. Keep cameras optional; silence and parallel work matter more than presence.

**Team failure modes:**

- The facilitator fills the silence with examples during generation.
- One person sketches one idea across all eight frames.
- Share becomes a pitch session and the quiet concepts never get read.
- Clustering by author or by team rather than by mechanism.
- Voting before every concept has been stated aloud.

## Preparing the prompt

A good Crazy Eights prompt is narrow enough to be answerable and open enough to admit different mechanisms:

- Weak: "Improve onboarding."
- Better: "How might we make a new analyst reach a first useful dashboard within their first day?"
- Also useful: "How might we make the first week feel like progress rather than setup?" — different moment, different success measure.

Write the prompt as a question, keep it under 20 words, and place it where it is visible during every slot.

## After the session

Same in every mode: freeze the eight, run the variety audit, cluster by mechanism, and harvest 2–3 concepts with probes. Assign an owner and a date to each probe. If no probe is scheduled, the session produced entertainment rather than learning.
