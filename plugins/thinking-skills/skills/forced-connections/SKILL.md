# Forced Connections

Familiar problems attract familiar associations. Ask for onboarding ideas and the answers are tours, checklists, and emails — variations on the domain's standard design. Forced connections breaks the cluster by importing a stimulus chosen with **no relation to the problem** (compost, a turnstile, a tide pool, escrow) and requiring its literal properties to mean something.

The forcing is the work. The stimulus is not a metaphor to admire or a domain to research. You extract properties, force them onto the problem, and keep only connections that name a mechanism and can be tested. The output is a small set of unexpected connections with their possible value explained — plus the ones you rejected for stated reasons.

## When to use

- Ideas are too similar; variants of one approach keep appearing.
- You need surprise rather than more analysis of the obvious answer.
- Familiar associations keep producing the same design.
- A team reuses one metaphor ("our air-traffic control") and needs a genuinely different frame.
- You have a defined problem and constraints and want departures from them, not a restatement.

If unsure whether this is the right method, start with [thinking-router](../thinking-router/SKILL.md) — or see [references/when-not-to-use.md](references/when-not-to-use.md).

## When NOT to use (brief)

- Another field may have solved the same *structure* and you want a transferable mechanism with limits → `analogical-thinking`.
- The blocker is an untested belief, not idea scarcity → `challenge-assumptions`.
- You need volume and breadth quickly → `brainwriting` or `divergent-thinking`.
- The task is correctness-bound (proof, safety, compliance) → vertical thinking.
- Facts are missing → research first; a stimulus cannot supply evidence.

## Process

1. **Intake.** In one sentence each: the problem, the goal behind it, hard constraints, baseline ideas already on the table, and appetite for weirdness (low / medium / high). The baseline matters — novelty is measured against it. Reuse context; ask only for gaps. Do not choose a stimulus that flatters a solution you already want.

2. **Choose the stimulus type.** Pick a **random word, object, image, or concept** by what you need: words are cheap and abstract, objects are affordance-rich, images resist vocabulary, concepts bring a developed mechanism. Use [references/stimuli.md](references/stimuli.md) for selection criteria and failure modes.

3. **Generate the stimulus.** Draw from an arbitrary source: the offline helper, a random page, an object on the desk, a captionless photo. Generate **3–5 candidates and pick one without curating for relevance**. If you can say in one sentence why the stimulus relates to your domain, reject it and redraw. Record the stimulus verbatim and its source.

4. **Force the connection.** First list **8–10 literal properties** of the stimulus — physical, functional, structural, temporal, relational — before interpreting it. Then map each property onto the problem and run an analogy sprint to push past the first obvious link. At least **three distinct properties** must be exercised. See [references/forcing.md](references/forcing.md).

5. **Extract insight.** Convert promising links into a mechanism: "If ⟨property⟩ held, the problem would change by ⟨causal path⟩." Score **novelty × usefulness** and kill connections that survive only as wordplay, mood, or unbounded fantasy. Keep 2–3. See [references/evaluation.md](references/evaluation.md).

6. **Probe.** For each keeper, define the smallest reversible test, the signal, the keep/adjust/abandon threshold, and the cheapest reason to stop. State what would falsify the mechanism. Worked examples: [references/examples.md](references/examples.md).

## Rules

- **Stimulus first, answer second.** If the "random" stimulus was chosen because it points at your preferred solution, the method has failed.
- **Literal before symbolic.** Properties must be true of the stimulus before they are suggestive about the problem.
- **Mechanism, not mood.** "Make onboarding feel like a compost heap" is not an idea. "Route resolved tickets through a weekly turning role" is.
- **Wordplay is not a connection.** Puns, rhymes, and shared vocabulary do not count as forcing; kill them.
- **Do not rescue bad connections.** Forcing is a generation instruction, not a claim that every link is valuable.
- **Variety is measured by mechanism**, not by how unusual the noun sounds.
- Label **fact / assumption / speculation**; the stimulus is not evidence about your problem.
- Honest provenance: de Bono's random entry and Synectics are lineages, not validations. See [references/provenance.md](references/provenance.md).
