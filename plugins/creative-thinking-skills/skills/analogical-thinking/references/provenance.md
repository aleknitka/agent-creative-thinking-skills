# Provenance — Lineage, Evidence, and Adaptations

## Lineage

- **Dedre Gentner — structure-mapping theory** (1983, "Structure-Mapping: A Theoretical Framework for Analogy"). Analogy is a mapping from a source to a target in which **relations**, especially higher-order relations, are preferred over object attributes. The **systematicity principle** holds that a connected system of relations is preferred over isolated matches. Candidate inferences are projected from source to target where the mapping supports them.
- **Keith Holyoak and Paul Thagard** (1989, 1995) — analogy as constraint satisfaction, with similarity, structure, and purpose guiding mapping. Their work with Gick on analogical transfer (1980, 1983) showed that people often fail to retrieve a useful source spontaneously, and that schema induction improves transfer.
- **Douglas Hofstadter and Emmanuel Sander — *Surfaces and Essences*** (2013) — analogy-making as central to cognition, not a special-purpose trick. This supports treating analogy as a general reasoning operation rather than a creativity game.
- **Mary Hesse and others in philosophy of science** — analogy as a source of candidate hypotheses, with the warning that analogies are heuristic, not proof.

This skill's six-step process is an operational adaptation of structure-mapping ideas, not a reproduction of any author's procedure.

## Adaptations in this skill

- The **function / flow / constraint / feedback** abstraction lenses and the structural statement template are practical scaffolding for step 2.
- The **domain prompt bank** and the **distance-is-structural** heuristic are original search aids for step 3.
- The **mapping table** and **breakpoint table** translate structure-mapping and candidate-inference ideas into a worksheet, with explicit holds / adapt / fails verdicts.
- The **principle + probe** output format is an adaptation aimed at agent workflows: a transferable claim with boundary conditions and a cheap falsification test.
- The two worked examples and their limit analyses are original.
- The helper script `scripts/analogy.py` is original and contains no proprietary text.

## Evidence and limits

- Analogy research is largely about human cognition. Controlled evidence supports the importance of relational structure and systematicity in mapping, and shows that **spontaneous retrieval of distant sources is unreliable** — surface similarity dominates, especially for novices. This is a reason for the skill's insistence on explicit abstraction and deliberate source search.
- There is limited experimental evidence that any specific facilitated analogy procedure improves organizational outcomes. Claims here are heuristic and should be tested in context.
- Analogical inference is defeasible: a mapping supports hypotheses, not conclusions. False inferences and overextension are documented failure modes, not edge cases.
- Expertise matters: people with deeper source knowledge map more accurately. A confidently described but inaccurate source is worse than an acknowledged guess.
- The method is weakest where the source's causal mechanism is poorly documented, where constraints differ radically, or where the problem cannot be structurally stated.
