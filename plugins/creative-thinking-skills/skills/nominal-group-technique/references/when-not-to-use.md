# When NOT to Use NGT or Delphi — and What to Use Instead

NGT is a **convergence** method: it takes a bounded set of options and produces
a defensible ranking. Delphi does the same asynchronously with anonymous expert
judgment. If the problem is still open, the options are not comparable, or no
one is authorised to act on the result, the protocol will add ceremony without
improving the decision.

## Quick triage

| Situation | Better fit | Why |
|---|---|---|
| You need more options, not a ranking | `brainwriting`, `divergent-thinking`, `crazy-eights` | NGT narrows; it does not widen the option set |
| A quick preference signal is enough | `dot-voting-prioritize` | Cheaper, visible, no silent-generation apparatus |
| The set is already clustered and the group is aligned | Plain dot voting or a criteria table | NGT's equal-airtime machinery is unnecessary |
| The decision must preserve a mix of safe and ambitious bets | `options-portfolio` | A single ranking collapses the portfolio into one winner |
| The top candidate needs stress-testing | `premortem` | NGT selects; it does not test failure modes |
| The question is vague ("improve onboarding") | `question-storming`, scoping, research | Ranking vague items produces a precise-sounding non-answer |
| The bottleneck is missing facts | Instrumentation, interviews, experiments | A vote cannot substitute for evidence |
| The decision is irreversible, safety-critical, or regulated | Governance, risk review, legal sign-off | A preference tally is not authority |
| The people affected are not in the voter set | Participatory process with affected users | Expert consensus is not representation |
| You need a statistically representative sample | Survey with sampling design | Delphi panels are purposive, not representative |

## NGT vs dot voting

Dot voting is the right default when the items are already on one board and the
group only needs a bounded, visible cut. NGT adds:

- silent generation before anyone speaks, so the item set itself is not anchored;
- a round-robin guarantee of airtime;
- a clarification stage that separates "I don't understand" from "I disagree";
- a two-ballot record that shows movement after discussion.

Those additions cost 60–90 minutes and facilitation discipline. Use NGT when
the ranking will be contested later, when hierarchy would distort a show of
dots, or when the group must own both the list and the order. Use dot voting
when the list is trusted and the decision is low-stakes or reversible.

## NGT vs brainwriting

Brainwriting generates, builds, and clusters; NGT ranks. They are sequential,
not interchangeable. Running NGT on an ungenerated list produces a tidy ranking
of whatever the facilitator already had in mind. Run divergence first when:

- fewer than about eight viable items exist;
- every item comes from the same person or function;
- the group has not yet named the mechanisms behind the problem.

Run NGT after clustering, not before. Clustering during NGT's clarification
stage should merge duplicates only, not discover new themes.

## NGT vs Delphi

| Condition | NGT | Delphi |
|---|---|---|
| Same-room or same-call availability | Yes | Not required |
| Need a decision in one session | Yes | No; allow weeks |
| Experts are dispersed or very senior | Hard to schedule | Strong fit |
| Anonymity is essential to candour | Partial; ballots can be secret, discussion is not | Strong fit |
| Response rate below ~70% is likely | Not applicable | Weak fit; report attrition or choose another method |
| The topic requires live negotiation | Yes | Poor fit; no channel for bargaining |

Do not run Delphi with a panel that cannot commit to multiple rounds. Do not
run NGT with a group so large that round-robin takes longer than the value of
the ranking; split into groups and reconcile, or use dot voting.

## Conditions that break the method

| Condition | Symptom | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| No frozen item set | New items appear during voting | Freeze and re-vote, or return to divergence |
| No decision owner | Ranking is admired and ignored | Name the owner and next action before starting |
| Senior leader campaigns | Ballots mirror the leader's view | Silent-first, fixed turns, secret ballot; consider an absent leader |
| Facilitator advocates | Clarification becomes a pitch | Separate facilitator from content owner |
| Visible early votes | Bandwagon or strategic voting | Hide results until the ballot closes |
| Delphi sponsor edits feedback | Panel converges on the sponsor's preference | Strip sponsor commentary; publish the rule |
| Panel is homogeneous | Fast, confident consensus | Diversify expertise and stake before Round 1 |
| Timebox too short | Clarification collapses into debate | Extend or use dot voting; do not skip silence |

## Handoff map

- **Need to package the top items as a balanced set of bets** →
  `options-portfolio`.
- **Need to stress-test the winner before commitment** → `premortem`.
- **Need a lighter, faster group cut after the list is trusted** →
  `dot-voting-prioritize`.
- **Need more items or new mechanisms** → `brainwriting`, `scamper`, or
  `forced-connections`, then return.
- **Need to decide whether the question is the right one** →
  `question-storming` or `thinking-router`.
- **Need a forecast rather than a preference** → use forecasting and evidence;
  Delphi can structure expert judgment, but it does not make the judgment
  accurate.

## The false-consensus trap

The most expensive misuse is treating a tight IQR or a unanimous ballot as
truth. NGT and Delphi control **process** — who speaks, when, with what
information — not the validity of the inputs. A panel of twelve people with the
same background can agree confidently on a wrong answer. Report consensus as a
description of the panel, keep the dissent log, and validate the top items
against evidence before committing resources.
