# Evaluating Forced Connections

Generation deliberately suspends judgment; this step supplies it. The question is not "is the connection interesting?" but "does the mechanism earn a test?" Most forced connections should die, and a good session kills them for stated reasons.

## Two axes

Score each candidate **novelty** and **usefulness** on 1–5. Do not collapse them into one score until you have looked at both.

| Score | Novelty | Usefulness |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Baseline idea; already on the table | Violates a hard constraint, or no mechanism stated |
| 2 | Slight variation on the baseline | Plausible mood, no causal path |
| 3 | Unusual for this team, but recognizable elsewhere | Fits constraints and names a mechanism, untested |
| 4 | No one in the room proposed it; requires a real shift | Fits constraints, mechanism is specific, a cheap probe exists |
| 5 | Reframes the problem, not just the solution | Fits constraints, mechanism is specific, and the probe has a clear signal and kill criterion |

## Decision rules

- **Keep:** novelty ≥3, usefulness ≥3, mechanism stated, probe designable.
- **Investigate:** novelty ≥4, usefulness 2 — sometimes cheap to test; cap the effort.
- **Kill:** usefulness 1, or mechanism absent regardless of novelty.
- **Kill:** novelty 1 — a familiar idea with a strange noun is not a forced connection.
- **Park:** high on both but not testable this quarter; record why, do not count it as a result.

Keep **2–3** candidates. A list of twelve "interesting" connections has not been evaluated.

## Kill criteria

Kill the connection if any of these is true:

- **Wordplay.** It works because of a shared word, rhyme, or pun, not a mechanism.
- **False property.** The link depends on a property the stimulus does not have.
- **Substitution.** You could replace the stimulus with any noun and the idea would read the same — the stimulus did no work.
- **Unbounded fantasy.** It only works if budget, physics, regulation, or authority is ignored.
- **Hard-constraint violation.** It requires breaking a constraint that is genuinely fixed.
- **Roadmap restatement.** It is the baseline idea with new vocabulary.
- **Unfalsifiable.** No observable could distinguish success from failure.
- **Problem change.** It only works by redefining the problem into a different one.
- **Symbolic decoration.** The value is in the image or story, not in any change to the system.

Killing is not failure. A killed connection with a reason teaches where the domain's associations are and improves the next draw.

## Scoring template

| # | Connection | Property used | Novelty | Usefulness | Mechanism | Decision |

| 1 | Weekly turning role for resolved tickets | Periodic turning | 4 | 4 | Rotating editor converts nominations into patches | Keep — probe |

## Value statement

For each keeper, write one sentence of possible value and one sentence of risk:

> **Worth testing because** ⟨mechanism⟩ could improve ⟨metric⟩ under ⟨constraint⟩. **Risk:** ⟨what could make it fail or cost too much⟩. **Cheapest check:** ⟨probe⟩.

This is the "explanation of possible value" the method promises. If you cannot write it, the connection is not ready.

## Biases to watch

- **Novelty halo.** Unusual nouns feel more creative; ratings drift upward when the stimulus is vivid. Rate usefulness first, then novelty.
- **Ownership bias.** The person who drew the stimulus defends it. Have someone else score, or score anonymously.
- **Facilitator curation.** A stimulus chosen for its fit produces high usefulness and low novelty; check the draw record.
- **Strained-link politeness.** Groups keep connections to avoid embarrassing the proposer. Enforce the kill criteria, not consensus.
- **One-axis optimization.** Usefulness alone returns the roadmap; novelty alone returns word salad.

## When nothing survives

That is a legitimate result. Record the kills and reasons, then:

1. Redraw a stimulus of a different type (word → object or concept).
2. Check whether the problem is idea-scarce or evidence-scarce; if the latter, stop and research.
3. If two sessions produce only baseline ideas, the constraint set may be the real blocker — hand off to `challenge-assumptions` or `first-principles`.

Do not lower the usefulness bar to justify the exercise.
