# When NOT to Use Liberating Structures — and What to Use Instead

These structures are containers for **equal-voice group work**. When the blocker is analysis, authority, facts, or safety, a structure will produce a well-formatted version of the same problem.

## Quick triage

| Your situation | Better fit | Why |
|---|---|---|
| One analyst can answer the question with data | Do the analysis; use a structure only to review it | Equal voice does not substitute for evidence |
| The decision is already made | Say so and take questions | A participation ritual without decision rights corrodes trust |
| Acute conflict, harassment, or no psychological safety | Mediation, HR, or a facilitated conflict process | A structure will amplify the conflict, not contain it |
| Narrow technical question needing depth | Expert review, a design review | Averaging experts is worse than consulting them |
| The problem is vague ("improve onboarding") | Scope it, `question-storming`, or `double-diamond` | A crisp question is a precondition for every structure here |
| You need many independent ideas with build passes | `brainwriting` | Deeper divergence with more rounds and explicit builds |
| You need to evaluate options against criteria | Decision matrix, `options-portfolio` | Facilitation produces input; it does not score |
| You need a rigorous failure analysis of one design | `inversion`, `premortem`, `reverse-brainstorming` | TRIZ is the social version; the analytical versions are sharper |
| You need futures or scenarios | `scenario-planning`, `futures-wheel`, `backcasting` | Structures surface present tensions; they do not model futures |
| The group is two or three people | Direct conversation | Structures add overhead without adding voices |
| Time is under 10 minutes and the issue is complex | Pick one structure and cut scope, or postpone | Compressing a structure removes the silent and pair rounds, which are the mechanism |

## Structural failures the method cannot fix

- **No decision rights.** If the group cannot change anything it names, the harvest becomes a complaint list. Fix the mandate or narrow the question to what the group controls.
- **Attendance by proxy.** People sent to "represent" a function defend positions rather than think. Invite the people who do the work.
- **Seniority in the room with veto power.** A structure can hold a hierarchy at bay for 40 minutes; it cannot survive an explicit veto at the harvest. Agree before the session what the senior person will and will not decide.
- **Language and accessibility gaps.** Silent writing and pair rounds help, but a group without a shared language needs interpretation and longer timeboxes, not a faster structure.
- **Safety.** If speaking up has cost people before, no timebox will fix it. Address the cost first.

## Liberating Structures vs. brainwriting

| | 1-2-4-All | Brainwriting |
|---|---|---|
| Medium | Speech in scaled groups | Writing, multiple passes |
| Time | 12–20 minutes | 30–90 minutes |
| Output | Themes and a shared picture | Many ideas plus labelled builds |
| Independence | Protected for one silent minute, then social | Protected across all generation passes |
| Best for | A meeting that needs breadth and convergence now | A campaign that needs volume and combination |

Use 1-2-4-All inside a meeting; use brainwriting when the idea space itself is the deliverable.

## Liberating Structures vs. six thinking hats

- **Six thinking hats** separates modes of thought (facts, feelings, caution, benefits, creativity, process) about one question. It is a sequencing discipline for evaluation and exploration.
- **Wicked Questions** holds two true commitments in one question. It is a framing discipline for tensions that cannot be resolved.
- If the group mixes criticism into brainstorming, use hats. If the group keeps flip-flopping between two legitimate priorities, use Wicked Questions.

## Liberating Structures vs. TRIZ contradictions

- `triz-contradictions` is the engineering method: identify a technical contradiction and apply inventive principles. It needs a parameterised system and produces design concepts.
- LS-TRIZ is a facilitation method: design the worst outcome, then stop the practices that produce it. It produces organisational commitments.
- Do not label a stop list "TRIZ" in an engineering review, and do not run inventive-principle analysis on a team's meeting habits.

## Anti-patterns

- Running a structure to "get buy-in" for a predetermined answer.
- Using 1-2-4-All as a faster round-robin: no silent minute, no quads, no synthesis.
- Letting the facilitator harvest by theme before reading raw entries, which erases dissent.
- Chaining four structures with no harvest between them; the group experiences activity, not progress.
- Treating the artifact as the outcome. The outcome is a decision, an owner, a date, and evidence.
- Calling any small-group exercise a "liberating structure" without the equal-voice and timebox rules.
- Running TRIZ when the group is not safe enough to admit the practices it already runs.

## Handoff triggers

| Next | Trigger |
|---|---|
| `brainwriting` | The harvest has themes but too few distinct ideas |
| `options-portfolio` | Several candidate moves and a need to balance the set |
| `premortem` | A move is chosen and commitment is approaching |
| `backcasting` | The group agrees on a destination but not the path |
| `stakeholder-perspectives` | The harvest reveals groups whose view is missing |
| `causal-loop-diagram` | The same stop list keeps regenerating because of a feedback loop |
| Pilot / experiment | A 15 percent action has a first step and a success signal |
| Decision meeting | The harvest is complete and the decision right sits elsewhere |
