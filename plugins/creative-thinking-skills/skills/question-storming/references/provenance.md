# Provenance

Question storming as practiced here is a synthesis of several traditions
about problem formulation. None of the named sources below owns the exact
six-step process in this skill; that process is an adaptation for agent and
team use.

## Lineage

**Question Burst (Hal Gregersen).** Gregersen, a leadership researcher at
MIT Sloan, developed the "Question Burst" exercise: a small group generates
questions about a challenge for four minutes, with no answers or explanations
permitted, then reflects on which questions open new paths. It is described in
"Better Brainstorming" (*Harvard Business Review*, March–April 2018) and in
the book *Questions Are the Answer* (2018, foreword by Ed Catmull). The core
mechanics this skill borrows are the strict question-only rule, the short time
box, deferred evaluation, and the claim that questions precede useful answers.

**The Innovator's DNA (Jeff Dyer, Hal Gregersen, Clayton Christensen).** This
work names questioning as one of five discovery skills of innovative leaders,
alongside observing, networking, experimenting, and associating. It is the
source of the framing that good questions are a learnable practice rather than
a personality trait.

**Question Formulation Technique (Dan Rothstein and Luz Santana, Right
Question Institute).** QFT specifies a disciplined sequence: produce as many
questions as possible, categorize open and closed questions, improve them,
and prioritize. Its rules — ask many, do not judge, capture verbatim, change
statements into questions — directly inform the generation and prioritization
steps here.

**Design thinking / IDEO.** The "How might we …" stem comes from design
practice popularized by IDEO and the Stanford d.school. It is used in this
skill as one generative stem among many, not as a universal format; it can
smuggle in a solution and should be used deliberately.

**Why-What's Stopping-How (Min Basadur).** Basadur's questioning sequence
("Why? What's stopping us? How?") is a compact reframing loop. It informs the
reframing and constraint categories, especially the move from a stated problem
to the goal above it and the obstacle beneath it.

**Problem formulation and "wicked problems."** Russell Ackoff's distinction
between doing the wrong thing well and doing the right thing, and Horst
Rittel and Melvin Webber's account of wicked problems, support the premise
that problem statements are choices with stakeholders, not neutral givens.
This skill only borrows that premise; it does not implement a full
problem-structuring method.

## What is adapted

- The six-category taxonomy (clarifying, reframing, stakeholder, constraint,
  causal, generative) is an organizing device for this skill. The sources
  above use different groupings.
- The three-axis ranking (leverage, tractability, reframing power) is an
  adaptation for decision support. Question Burst emphasizes reflection on
  which questions open new paths; QFT uses a simpler prioritization vote.
- The "revise the problem statement" output and the probe table are
  integrations from problem-structuring and evidence-based practice, not
  parts of Question Burst.
- The script and solo/AI facilitation guidance are original to this skill.

## Evidence limits

- Research on question-generation exercises is small-scale and mostly
  observational or practitioner-reported. Claims that a specific protocol
  reliably increases innovation or decision quality should be treated as
  plausible hypotheses, not established effects.
- Group brainstorming research generally finds that nominal groups (people
  generating independently, then pooling) outperform interacting groups in
  quantity and diversity of ideas. The silent-start and round-robin mechanics
  reflect that finding, but were not tested here for questions specifically.
- Facilitation effects depend on psychological safety and hierarchy. A
  question storm in a low-trust group can surface less than a private written
  exercise.
- "Better questions produce better outcomes" is the method's premise, not a
  measured law. Use the revision and probe steps to test whether the new
  question actually changes what you do.

## Attribution discipline

When teaching or adapting this skill, distinguish:

- the borrowed mechanic (e.g., four-minute question-only burst — Gregersen);
- the borrowed principle (e.g., statements to questions — QFT);
- the local adaptation (e.g., six categories, three-axis ranking).

Do not present the whole method as any single source's protocol.
