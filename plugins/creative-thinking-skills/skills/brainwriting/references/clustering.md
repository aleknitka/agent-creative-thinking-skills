# Clustering Written Ideas

A 6-3-5 sheet set produces 50–108 raw entries, many of them near-duplicates, fragments, and builds of other entries. Clustering turns that pile into a handful of themes a person can actually evaluate. Do it after the freeze, never during generation.

## Procedure

1. **Transcribe before sorting.** Assign every entry a global ID (`S2-R4a` = sheet 2, round 4, cell a). Keep the author's wording; fix only spelling that blocks reading.
2. **Strip the builds.** Separate entries labelled `build on #⟨id⟩` from standalone ideas. A build is evidence that the source idea resonated; record the edge, then judge the combined idea on its own.
3. **Merge exact and near duplicates.** Same mechanism, same actor, same outcome → one entry. Keep the sharpest wording and list the source IDs. Do not merge things that merely sound similar.
4. **Group by theme or mechanism.** Ask of each pair: *would a single intervention plausibly serve both?* If yes, same cluster. If they merely co-occur (both about onboarding), keep them apart.
5. **Name each cluster with a phrase, not a bucket.** "Make the first step observable" is a theme; "UX ideas" is a bucket. Names should imply what kind of response would serve the cluster.
6. **Count.** Expect **3–6 clusters** from 30–100 entries. More than six usually means clustering by wording; fewer than three usually means one theme has swallowed the list.
7. **Check coverage and provenance.** Does the set include entries from every participant and from both passes? A cluster made only of one person's entries is a signal, not necessarily a winner.
8. **Select only now.** Choose 2–3 clusters to elaborate. Prefer clusters that (a) aggregate several independent entries, (b) span different participants, and (c) contain at least one build edge — those are the ideas the group actually compounded.

## Grouping choices

| Basis | Good when | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanism ("what makes it work") | Ideas are solutions to the same problem | Requires judgement; can force dissimilar ideas together |
| Theme ("what it is about") | Early, messy lists; many unrelated entries | Buckets that imply nothing actionable |
| Actor/touchpoint ("who acts") | Service or process design | Hides cross-cutting mechanisms that span actors |
| Constraint ("what it relaxes") | Ideas challenge different constraints | Some entries relax several constraints |
| Time horizon | Mixing quick wins and bets | Can smuggle prioritisation into clustering |

Use **mechanism** as the default. It survives reorgs and rewording, and it feeds directly into concept writing.

## Output format

For each cluster record:

- **ID and name** — e.g., C2 "visible progress".
- **One-line theme** — what the member ideas have in common, in mechanism terms.
- **Member IDs** — the source entries, strongest wording kept.
- **Build edges** — which members were builds of which (the compounding trail).
- **Spread** — how many participants and passes contributed.
- **Tension** — any member that contradicts the cluster; do not silently drop it.
- **Candidate concept** — one phrase, not the full paragraph yet.

## From cluster to concept

A cluster is raw material; a concept is a decision-shaped sentence.

- **Bad:** "Improve reminders." (theme restated)
- **Better:** "Send a same-day SMS at 7am with a one-tap reschedule link, and escalate to a call if the patient has missed two appointments." (mechanism, actor, trigger)

Write each concept as: **⟨what changes⟩ for ⟨whom⟩ when ⟨trigger⟩, by ⟨mechanism⟩.** Keep the member IDs attached so the concept can be traced back to the written record.

## Traps

- **Clustering by author.** Turns the board into a scoreboard and buries ideas from quiet participants.
- **Clustering by department or phase.** Tells you who owns the fix, not why the idea works.
- **Voting before naming.** Counts reward familiar wording; name clusters first, then discuss.
- **Superclusters.** "Culture" or "better UX" absorbs everything and yields no probe.
- **Losing the odd entry.** A single entry that fits no cluster may be the most original idea; keep an "unclustered" pile and review it before closing.
- **Clustering during generation.** Any grouping visible on the board mid-pass anchors subsequent writers; keep categories out of sight until freeze.
- **Treating cluster size as quality.** Five variants of one obvious idea are still one idea.
