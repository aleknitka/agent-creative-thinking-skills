# Crazy Eights

Eight ideas, eight minutes, nothing edited. The clock is the method: fill all eight slots before the inner critic can narrow the field to one safe option.

The failure mode it prevents is spending an hour elaborating the first plausible idea and then calling it the plan.

## When to use

- You need a quick burst of alternatives before converging on a direction.
- The group keeps circling one concept; adjacent possibilities stay invisible.
- You are in an early divergent phase with a defined problem and a short window.
- You want eight comparable concepts to cluster and choose between.

If unsure this is the right method, start with [thinking-router](../thinking-router/SKILL.md) — or see [references/when-not-to-use.md](references/when-not-to-use.md).

## When NOT to use (brief)

- You need systematic coverage of a design space → `morphological-analysis`.
- You need volume without a fixed count or timebox → `brainwriting`, `divergent-thinking`.
- You have one baseline to improve → `scamper`.
- Facts about the problem or users are missing → research first, then return.
- Candidates already exist and the job is to choose → `options-portfolio`.

## Process

1. **Intake — reuse before you ask.** In one paragraph: the problem as a "How might we…" question, who it is for, what success means, hard constraints, and what has already been tried. Mark fact / assumption / speculation and ask only for gaps. A vague prompt produces eight versions of the same vague idea. See [references/procedure.md](references/procedure.md).

2. **Set up eight slots.** Draw or list frames 1–8, each with one minute attached. Put the prompt and constraints where they stay visible. In text, a slot is a place for a 1–2 sentence concept plus a mechanism label — not a title. See [references/procedure.md](references/procedure.md) and [scripts/eights.py](scripts/eights.py).

3. **Generate eight in eight.** One idea per slot, one minute per slot, no judging, no going back. If a slot stalls, start a deliberately bad or partial idea and keep moving; the next slot does not inherit it. Stop at eight even if a ninth is forming — write that one down separately. See [references/procedure.md](references/procedure.md).

4. **Share and note variety.** Read the eight aloud or in sequence without defending them. For each, name the mechanism in 3–6 words and what it changes versus the others. Mark claims that are assumptions. This is the first judgment pass — and only about difference, not quality. See [references/variety.md](references/variety.md).

5. **Cluster.** Group concepts that share a mechanism, not a topic or a phrase. Name each cluster by the mechanism it represents; leave genuine singletons alone. A cluster of one is a finding, not a failure. See [references/variety.md](references/variety.md).

6. **Harvest top 2–3 + probe.** Pick a portfolio, not a winner: typically one safe, one ambitious, and one cheap-and-strange concept. Restate each in 2–3 sentences with a concrete mechanism and its strongest assumption, then give each the smallest next probe — a prototype, a user conversation, a fake-door test, an estimate — with the signal that would change your mind. See [references/examples.md](references/examples.md) and [references/facilitation.md](references/facilitation.md).

## Rules

- The timebox is the mechanism — do not borrow minutes from later slots.
- Fill all eight slots. Quantity before quality; a weak slot still teaches you where the space is thin.
- Variety must be mechanistic: a reworded version of an earlier concept does not count as a new slot.
- Separate generation from evaluation. No ranking, feasibility checks, or "yes, but" until all eight exist.
- In text, every slot needs a concrete mechanism; "better onboarding" is a label, not a concept.
- Label facts, assumptions, and speculation. Eight concepts are hypotheses, not validated options.
- Honest provenance: attribute Crazy Eights to Knapp/Google Ventures; label the text adaptation as an adaptation. See [references/provenance.md](references/provenance.md).
