# Variety That Counts — Mechanisms, Not Wording

Eight slots are only worth more than one if they explore different mechanisms. A page of near-synonyms is a single idea with eight labels, and it will produce a confident but narrow shortlist.

## The test

Ask of each new slot: **what would have to change in the world for this concept to work, and is that different from the previous one?**

- Different actor, incentive, trigger, artifact, or removed constraint → different mechanism.
- Different adjective, channel synonym, or phrasing of the same move → not a new slot.

Examples:

- "A guided checklist" → "A step-by-step wizard" is wording. Both are a prescribed sequence.
- "A guided checklist" → "An onboarding buddy who does the setup for you" changes the actor and the division of labour. That is a mechanism.
- "Email nudges" → "SMS nudges" is a channel variation; it counts only if the channel change alters behaviour (e.g., reaching people who never open email).
- "Freemium trial" → "reverse trial" is a mechanism change: the default experience and the downgrade moment both change.

## Axes worth spreading across

Use these as lenses during the "differs from" field, not as a mandatory template:

- **Actor:** who does the work — user, colleague, machine, community, third party.
- **Trigger:** when it happens — at signup, at first failure, at a time boundary, on a signal, on request.
- **Form:** what the user encounters — conversation, artifact, sequence, default, marketplace, game, contract.
- **Incentive:** what is rewarded or made costly — time, money, status, access, reciprocity.
- **Constraint removed:** which assumed constraint is dropped — no account, no setup, no training, no approval, no cost.
- **Tempo:** one-off, recurring, on demand, always-on.
- **Locus:** in-product, human service, physical world, ecosystem.

A useful eight-slot set usually touches at least four axes.

## A variety audit (two minutes, after generation)

For each pair of slots, write one of:

- **M** — different mechanism.
- **V** — variation on the same mechanism (different wording, channel, or surface).
- **D** — duplicate.

Then answer:

1. How many distinct mechanisms are present? Fewer than four is a thin set.
2. Which axis is over-represented? If six slots are in-product features, the set has a blind spot, not a breadth result.
3. Which slot is the outlier? Keep it. Outliers are often the only evidence that the space extends further than the team's habits.
4. Which pair could be merged without losing anything? Merge it in clustering, not during generation.

## Forced variety seeds

When the first six slots cluster tightly, use the last slots deliberately:

- **Opposite:** invert the dominant mechanism. If everything is add-a-feature, remove a step.
- **Different actor:** hand the job to someone or something else.
- **Constraint-free:** design as if budget, law, or legacy systems did not exist; note which constraint you suspended.
- **Borrowed:** copy a mechanism from another industry, then adapt it to this problem.
- **Extreme:** set a parameter to 10× or 1/10 (price, speed, frequency, scope).
- **Boring baseline:** the do-nothing or keep-as-is option. It is a real comparator and often the strongest probe.

These seeds are prompts, not a requirement to produce one of each. Their job is to break the run of variations, not to manufacture novelty.

## False variety

Watch for these, which look diverse and are not:

- **Format swaps:** same concept as a dashboard, an email, and a chatbot.
- **Segment swaps:** same concept for three user types, with no change in mechanism.
- **Fake extremes:** "what if onboarding were a game" with no rule change — the game framing decorates the same sequence.
- **Constraint theatre:** claiming "no budget" while keeping every other assumption that depends on budget.
- **Renaming:** the mechanism label is new; the "differs from" field says "same, but clearer".

If the eight survive the audit with four or more distinct mechanisms, proceed to clustering. If not, run a second eight-minute pass explicitly seeded with the missing axes rather than polishing the existing eight.

## Variety versus feasibility

Do not let "could we actually build it?" collapse the set during generation. Keep the eight mixed: some obviously feasible, some doubtful, at least one that would require a capability you do not have. The portfolio step exists to choose across that spread; the generation step exists to make the spread visible.

At the same time, do not confuse illegality or physical impossibility with creativity. A concept that violates a hard constraint is not a useful alternative unless the point of the slot was to question that constraint — and then say so explicitly.
