# When NOT to Use Bodystorming — and What to Use Instead

## Quick triage

| Your situation | Better fit | Why |
|---|---|---|
| No spatial or service interaction to enact | `what-if` or `challenge-assumptions` | Problem is conceptual, not embodied |
| Team cannot access space or props | `question-storming` or `abstraction-laddering` | Enactment needs a footprint |
| A quick assumption test decides the question | Experiment or probe directly | Bodystorming is heavier than a test |
| You need structured variations on a baseline | `scamper` or `morphological-analysis` | Systematic variation, not enactment |
| You need to choose among known candidates | `options-portfolio` or `premortem` | Convergence, not divergence |
| You need stakeholder tradeoffs broadly | `stakeholder-perspectives` | Social mapping without enactment |

## Don't confuse

- **Bodystorming vs. Roleplay / Service Walkthrough:** bodystorming is low-fidelity, body-first, and discovery-oriented; roleplay can be scripted and evaluative. Use bodystorming to find gaps, roleplay to test a handoff.
- **Bodystorming vs. Prototyping:** bodystorming surfaces spatial and timing gaps before any prototype is built. Do not build first and then enact to validate.

## Handoff triggers

When bodystorming isn't the right entry point, triage with `thinking-router` (*better question / more ideas / broader perspectives*).

Bodystorming hands off to:

| Next | Trigger |
|---|---|
| `stakeholder-perspectives` | Enactment revealed an overlooked group |
| `constraint-driven-ideation` | Space revealed a keepable constraint |
| `premortem` | You selected a spatial change and need to harden |

## Anti-patterns

- Enacting at desk scale — the gaps that matter are at real scale.
- Keeping original roles — the designer must experience the user role.
- Building realistic props — fidelity hides improvisation.
