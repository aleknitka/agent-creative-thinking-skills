# Provenance — Attribution, Origin, and Evidence

## Named lineage

- **Systematic Inventive Thinking (SIT)** — developed by **Roni Horowitz and Jacob Goldenberg** from Altshuller's TRIZ, formalizing five closed-world operators (Hatchuel & Weil's C-K Theory provides a related design-theoretic framing; see the extended-methods synthesis). SIT emphasizes the *Closed World* and *Qualitative Change* conditions: do not import new kinds of resources, and the result must behave differently.
- **SIT + GenAI bridge:** 2024 OpenAlex hit `W4407721440` examines generative AI support for SIT processes; earlier SIT training work is captured in `W2753361279` (2017).

## Adaptations in this skill

- The five-operator recipes, the closed-world facilitation rule, and the virtual-product → enrichment → probe workflow are practical adaptations for AI-assisted and workshop use.
- Call adaptations adaptations when attributing externally.

## Evidence and limits

- Controlled evaluations of SIT as a standalone protocol are limited and training-dependent; the 2024 SIT+GenAI and 2017 SIT/SRL studies report learning and invention support but with variable transfer. This skill is presented as a disciplined heuristic for constrained generation, not as an empirically validated intervention for all domains.
- Tag evidence quality for probes (fact vs. assumption vs. speculation) and make probes owner- and date-bound.
- For sharing externally, cite the method by name and edition and note any adaptation.
