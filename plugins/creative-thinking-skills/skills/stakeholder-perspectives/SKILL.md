# Stakeholder Perspective-Taking

The same design looks different from each corner it touches. This skill maps who is affected, what they need and can bear, and where a fix for one creates a cost for another.

AI-generated perspectives are hypotheses to validate — not user research.

The failure mode it prevents is optimizing for one stakeholder while externalizing the cost to those who were not in the room.

## When to use

- A solution works for one group but may burden another.
- Overlooked groups may be affected.
- You need tensions and tradeoff opportunities, not just a feature list.

If unsure this is the right method, start with [thinking-router](../thinking-router/SKILL.md) — or see [references/when-not-to-use.md](references/when-not-to-use.md).

## When NOT to use (brief)

- Users have already been directly consulted → use their findings, not this scaffold.
- You need many ideas at once → `divergent-thinking`.
- You need to choose among many → `options-portfolio`.

## Process

1. **Intake — reuse before you ask.** State the problem, the proposed direction, and who is already considered. Mark fact / assumption / speculation. See [references/stakeholders.md](references/stakeholders.md).

2. **Map stakeholders.** List primary, secondary, and overlooked groups — including those who pay the cost but have no voice in the decision. Include at least one overlooked group explicitly. See [references/stakeholders.md](references/stakeholders.md) and [scripts/stakeholders.py](scripts/stakeholders.py).

3. **Surface needs and constraints.** For each group, name what they need, what they fear, and what constrains them. Keep constraints observable (time, cost, skill, access), not assumed attitudes.

4. **Name tensions.** For each pair, state the tension as a tradeoff: improving X for group A worsens Y for group B. Tag each tension as real or assumed.

5. **Find better-tradeoff opportunities.** Generate designs that ease a tension without fully satisfying either side — re-scope, re-allocate, or change who bears what cost.

6. **Prioritize and probe.** Select 2–3 tensions to address first; define one probe per tension that would reveal whether the tradeoff is real and whether the design eases it. See [references/examples.md](references/examples.md).

## Rules

- Include at least one often-overlooked group — if the map omits them, it reproduces the blind spot.
- Perspectives are hypotheses — validate with real input; do not present them as findings.
- Every tension is a tradeoff with two sides; one-sided complaints are not tensions.
- Label fact / assumption / speculation.
- Honest provenance: call adaptations adaptations. See [references/provenance.md](references/provenance.md).
