# The Seven SCAMPER Operators — Prompts, Triggers, and Examples

Each operator turns the baseline into variations by asking what to change. Work one operator at a time; no judging or clustering until after generation.

> Checklist due to Alex F. Osborn (*Applied Imagination*, 1953) as 73 questions; compressed into the SCAMPER acronym by Bob Eberle (*SCAMPER: Games for Imagination Development*, 1971). See Provenance for attribution.

## S — Substitute — what can be replaced with something else?

**Core move:** Swap a material, component, role, channel, or technology.

- What material, ingredient, or component could be used instead?
- Which process step could be replaced by a different one?
- Which human touchpoint could become digital (or vice versa)?
- Which channel (phone, email, chat) or pricing mechanism (fixed, subscription, pay-per-use) could replace the current one?

Examples: replace the annual paper policy letter with a personalized video summary; replace separate PIN mailing with digital confirmation.

## C — Combine — what can be merged with something else?

**Core move:** Bundle functions, services, touchpoints, or data.

- Which two separate steps or touchpoints could become one?
- Which services from different industries could be bundled?
- Which internal processes could be merged so the customer feels a seamless flow?
- Which data from different systems could be combined for new value?

Examples: account opening + online banking activation + app setup in a single guided session; checking + savings + credit card as a one-click starter bundle.

## A — Adapt — what can be borrowed or adapted from elsewhere?

**Core move:** Import a solution, analogy, or best practice from another field.

- Which solution from another industry solves a similar problem?
- Which analogy from nature, art, or technology could inspire?
- Which customer experience from hospitality, e-commerce, or gaming could be adapted?
- How does a completely different industry handle this same friction?

Examples: adapt real-time parcel tracking to technician arrival ("your technician arrives at 10:42"); adapt social-network progress bars to onboarding completion.

## M — Modify / Magnify / Minimize — what can be enlarged, reduced, or toned down?

**Core move:** Change size, scope, frequency, or intensity.

- What happens if we double or halve the frequency?
- What happens if we shorten response time from 24 hours to 1 hour?
- What happens if we radically expand or strip the scope to essentials?
- What happens if we massively increase personalization or narrow to the top 10% of customers?

Examples: reduce onboarding from 14 days to 14 minutes via an interactive single-session tutorial; expand service scope to a concierge chat in week one.

## P — Put to other use — what else could this (or part of it) be used for?

**Core move:** Repurpose the service, its parts, byproducts, or data.

- Are there other target groups or contexts where this provides value?
- Which data collected during the service could be offered as standalone value?
- Could an internal training process or tool be offered externally?
- Could the service be valuable in a completely different industry?

Examples: anonymized delivery data offered to urban planners as a traffic-flow data service; internal training portal repurposed as a customer learning product.

## E — Eliminate — what can be removed, simplified, or omitted?

**Core move:** Cut steps, features, or touchpoints that no longer earn their keep.

- Which process step is superfluous or historical?
- Which feature is used by fewer than 10% of users?
- Which form, input, or approval could be replaced by automatic data transfer?
- What could we leave out without losing core value?

Examples: eliminate the in-person signature for standard contracts under a threshold; eliminate separate PIN mailing via digital setup in-app; replace a 4-page form with a 3-click confirmation using pre-filled data.

## R — Reverse / Rearrange — what happens if we flip the order or swap roles?

**Core move:** Reverse sequence, invert timing, or swap who does what.

- What happens if we reverse the order of steps?
- What happens if the customer configures the service instead of the company?
- What happens if we deliver at the customer's location instead of ours?
- What happens if we shift from reactive to proactive (offer before the problem occurs)?

Examples: proactive fault detection that notifies customers before they notice; customer choosing which 3 of 8 services to explore first instead of the company prescribing.

## Quality checks before you cluster

- Did you keep to one operator at a time?
- Is each idea a distinct mechanism or constraint change, not a rewording?
- Can you state the idea without buzzwords — who does what, with what input and output?

## Choosing a subset (selective mode)

For experienced teams short on time, pick the 3–4 most relevant operators for your goal:

- Legacy process with bloat → prioritize **E** and **S** (highest ROI in established services).
- Need integration → prioritize **C** and **R**.
- Need differentiation → prioritize **A** and **M**.
