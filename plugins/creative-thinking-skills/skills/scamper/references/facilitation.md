# Facilitation — Preparation, Execution, and Follow-up

SCAMPER works when the subject is concrete and the pass has depth. Rushing all 7 operators superficially produces platitudes.

## Preparation (10 min)

- **Define the subject** so concretely you can describe it in 3–5 sentences: what steps it includes, who is involved, what triggers it, what the outcome is. "Our customer service" is too broad; "the callback process for customer complaints" is usable.
- **Document the current state:** 3–5 sentence baseline + what works and what doesn't.
- **Prepare participants:** 4–8 people, ideally cross-functional. Share the baseline and operator list in advance so warm-up is brief.

## Subject checklist (must pass before you start)

- Can you name the trigger and outcome in one sentence each?
- Can you list the steps or touchpoints (3–7 bullets)?
- Do you know who is involved (roles, not names)?
- Do you know what hurts or is wasted today?

If any answer is "not yet," narrow the subject — don't start SCAMPER on a vague topic.

## Execution — three modes

### A. Sequential (beginners, 30–45 min)

Work through all 7 operators in order. Per operator: 4–5 min silent brainwriting (everyone notes ideas), then 2 min sharing. No clustering until all operators are done.

### B. Selective (experienced teams, 25–35 min)

Choose the 3–4 most relevant operators for your goal. Spend 8–10 min per operator. Deeper ideas; less fatigue.

### C. Rotation (larger groups, 45–60 min)

Each table works on a different operator. After 10 min, groups rotate and build on the previous table's ideas. Good for building on others' creativity.

## Generation rules (during execution)

- One operator at a time — no jumping or clustering.
- Silent brainwriting first — everyone writes before anyone speaks.
- Quantity with depth — 2–3 ideas per operator per person is a good target; prefer elaborated mechanisms over one-line labels.
- Tag claims as you go: fact vs. assumption vs. speculation.

## Follow-up (15–20 min) — don't skip

1. **Cluster** ideas thematically.
2. **Evaluate** with dot-voting or a 2×2 (innovation potential × feasibility).
3. **Select top 2–3** concepts — keep a portfolio (not just the safest); elaborate each into a 1–2 sentence concept with a concrete mechanism.
4. **Define next steps:** who develops which idea by when, and what is the first probe (prototype, stakeholder check, feasibility spike).

## Adapting for solo or human-AI use

- Solo: run brainwriting as timed solo passes per operator, then self-cluster.
- With AI: the AI brainwrites alongside you — it does not cluster or evaluate until you do.
- If no installed skill matches a follow-up need, use the handoff map in the companion reference — also indexed from the skill's main guide.

## Common anti-patterns to avoid here

- Rushing all 7 operators in 30 min → 1–2 shallow ideas per operator.
- Starting with a vague subject → ideas remain arbitrary.
- Skipping follow-up → 25 sticky notes and nothing happens.
