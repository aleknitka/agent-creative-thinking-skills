# Worked Example — Clinic Capacity → Rich Picture → CATWOE → Probe

Situation: community clinic at capacity; missed appointments rise when demand spikes.

## Rich picture (key elements)

- Actors: patients (new/returning), front-desk, care team, community health workers, payers.
- Flows: referral → booking → reminder → arrival → visit → follow-up; information re-entered at check-in; no signal for queue length.
- Tensions: staff re-entry vs. patient waiting; payer wants throughput vs. care team wants continuity.
- Concerns (thought bubbles): patient — "I waited and still got turned away"; staff — "We rescue at the door because we cannot see the queue."

## CATWOE for one system

- **C:** patients seeking a visit (observable: missed vs. kept appointment).
- **A:** front-desk + care team.
- **T:** queue → served case.
- **W:** W = visibility of the queue predicts rescue without harming continuity.
- **O:** clinic operations lead (can stop or rescope the queue change).
- **E:** limited EHR change window; 70% smartphone / 25% no phone; six weeks.

## Root definition

"A system owned by the operations lead, operated by front-desk + care team, to transform queue into served case for patients, under worldview W = queue visibility predicts rescue, within constraints of limited EHR change and mixed phone access."

## Debate

Returning patients picture continuity differently than new patients; W is contested — do queued patients actually want a same-day rescue? Dissent: new patients assume walk-in is possible.

## Probes

- Instrument queue length and wait time for one site, four weeks; signal: time-to-rescue vs. baseline.
- Small test: visible queue signal (one card per queued patient) and pre-visit arrival cue; signal: rescue rate without added headcount.
