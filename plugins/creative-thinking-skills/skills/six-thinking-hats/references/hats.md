# The Six Hats — Modes, Prompts, and Pitfalls

Hats are **thinking modes**, not personality types. Everyone wears the same hat at the same time.

> Method due to Edward de Bono — *Six Thinking Hats* (1985). Condensed summary; see Provenance for full attribution.

## At a glance

| Hat | Mode | Core question | What you produce | Tag claims as |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ⚪ **White** | Information | What do we know / not know / need? | Facts, gaps, questions | fact / gap / assumption |
| 🔴 **Red** | Feelings & intuition | What do we feel / sense / like? | Gut reactions, hunches | subjective (no justification) |
| 🟡 **Yellow** | Benefits & value | Where does this work even if imperfect? | Benefits, conditions for success | assumption / speculation |
| ⚫ **Black** | Caution & risks | What could go wrong? | Risks, weak points, checks needed | fact or assumption (be specific) |
| 🟢 **Green** | Creativity & alternatives | What else could we try? | New ideas, provocations, modifications | speculation (then probe) |
| 🔵 **Blue** | Process control | How should we think about this? | Focus, sequence, time-boxes, synthesis | — |

## Per-hat prompts and pitfalls

### ⚪ White — Information

- Prompts: What do we know for sure? What's missing? What would we need to verify?
- Output: bullet list of knowns, unknowns, and next data to collect.
- Pitfall: drifting into interpretation ("this means…") — save that for Yellow/Black.
- Transition cue: "We have enough to share hunches — Red next."

### 🔴 Red — Feelings & intuition

- Prompts: What's your gut reaction? What do you like/dislike about this, without defending it?
- Output: 1–2 sentence reactions per person; no debate, no justification.
- Pitfall: demanding reasons for feelings — that belongs to White/Black. Keep Red brief (1–2 min).
- Label: every Red statement is subjective by definition.

### 🟡 Yellow — Benefits and value

- Prompts: Where does value appear even if the idea is flawed? Who benefits, when?
- Output: specific benefits with their conditions ("Value X appears if condition Y holds").
- Pitfall: cheerleading without conditions — Yellow must name the *when*, not just the *what*.
- Note: Yellow is not optimism as mood; it is deliberate value-seeking.

### ⚫ Black — Caution and risks

- Prompts: What is weak, risky, or costly? What must be checked before committing?
- Output: specific risks with likelihood/impact hints, not blanket dismissal.
- Pitfall: performing skepticism ("it'll never work") instead of naming testable concerns.
- Pair: Black after Yellow ensures caution is not confused with negativity.

### 🟢 Green — Creativity and alternatives

- Prompts: What new approach could serve the same goal? What provocation would break the pattern?
- Output: raw alternatives, provocations (`Po:`), modifications — quantity before quality.
- Pitfall: judging ideas as they appear — no Black during Green. Lateral moves belong here.
- If stuck: pull a provocation operator from `lateral-thinking` or use the random-entry picker.

### 🔵 Blue — Process control

- Prompts: What's our focus? Which sequence fits the goal and time? Are we keeping one hat at a time?
- Output: agenda, time-boxes, sequence choice, and final synthesis.
- Pitfall: letting Blue become another content hat — Blue manages *how* we think, not *what*.

## How hats are called

- Blue opens ("We'll go White → Red → Yellow → Black → Green → Blue") and closes.
- Any participant may request a hat switch ("Blue request: we need White data before more Green").
- In human-AI use, the AI wears the called hat too — it does not stay neutral.

## Quality checks per hat

- Is everyone wearing the same hat?
- Is the output tagged (fact / assumption / speculation / subjective)?
- Was the time-box respected, or did Blue explicitly extend it?
