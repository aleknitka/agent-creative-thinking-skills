# Concept Space (C) — Partitions and the Design Square

## What a concept is

A concept is a proposition with **no logical status** in current Knowledge — you cannot prove it true or false from what you already know. "An onboarding that needs no welcome email" is a concept; "An onboarding with a welcome email" is an instance.

## Partitions

- **Restrictive partition:** adds a property that already has precedent in K (e.g., "without welcome email, but with invite"). Refines within known knowledge.
- **Expansive partition:** adds a property with **no precedent** in K — it forces a K expansion. Example: "without any push message at all" when K has no such onboarding.

Keep at least one expansive partition per round; restrictive-only trees are morphological refinements, not C-K expansions.

## The design square

Draw Concept expansions on one axis, Knowledge expansions on the other. Record each round as one C partition and the K pocket it calls. A growing square shows generativity; a flat row shows variation without learning.

## Concept vs. requirement

A requirement is decidable ("must send email within 24h"). A concept is undecidable ("without push, still succeeds"). If the starting phrase is decidable, restate it as a concept before partitioning.

## Selecting branches

Keep expansive branches that create a new K pocket with learning value — even if they seem distant. Discard paths that merely refine the initial concept without calling new knowledge.
