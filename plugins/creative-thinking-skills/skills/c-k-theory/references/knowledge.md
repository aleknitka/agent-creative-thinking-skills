# Knowledge Space (K) — Pockets, Activation, and Learning Agenda

## What K contains

Verified facts, tested methods, measured behaviors, and domain knowledge that can decide a concept. K is not opinion — every element must be testable or learnable.

Examples: activation delay data, invite open rates, push deliverability by source, teammate artifact usage.

## Pockets and activation

- A **knowledge pocket** is the cluster that would decide one expansive partition.
- **Activation** means naming the pocket that an expansive concept calls — or naming the gap where no pocket exists.

If pocket exists and can be applied, add it to K and mark the concept as partially decided. If no pocket exists, record the **learning agenda**: what must be learned, from whom, by when.

## Learning vs. speculation

Adding speculation to K collapses C-K into brainstorming. Keep K verifiable — an entry with no learning path is not K, it is still C.

## K expansion and generativity

Activating a pocket often suggests new C partitions ("now that we know invite intent predicts activation, we can consider a concept where onboarding is entirely invite-driven"). Record that as the next C→C operation; the design square grows on both axes.

## Probes and ownership

After expansion, each kept branch points to one K-expansion and one probe — the smallest test of the pocket that would decide it. Tag ownership and date.
