# Challenge Starter — Exposing the Dominant Idea

Before you provoke, make the dominant idea explicit. This is the "main track" everyone is optimizing. Most of it was never decided — it just accumulated.

Use this as a checklist in skill step 2. Pick 4–6 questions that fit; stop when the dominant idea is nameable in one sentence.

## 1. Name the dominant idea

- If you had to state the current approach as a slogan, what would it be?
- What would an outsider say "this team believes" after 5 minutes of listening?
- What pattern keeps getting reused even when it fails?

> Write it as: **"We are optimizing _X_ by doing _Y_ because we assume _Z_."**

## 2. Challenge assumptions — why does this exist?

- Why does this step/role/constraint exist? What was the original reason?
- Which assumptions are inherited ("we've always done it this way") vs. proven constraints?
- What would make this assumption false, unnecessary, or reversed?
- What do we treat as fixed that a newcomer would question?

## 3. Challenge continuity — does the sequence have to be this way?

- Does the order have to be this order? What if it ran backwards or in parallel?
- Where does feedback actually land vs. where it is needed?
- Which step exists only because the previous step exists?

## 4. Challenge boundaries — where did we draw the box?

- What did we include/exclude without noticing? (People, time, cost, data, channel.)
- Is the boundary the real constraint, or a convenient one?
- Nine Dots reminder: the "square" was never in the instructions — what is our invisible square?

## 5. Challenge framing — who defines the problem?

- Who framed the problem this way, and who would frame it differently?
- What would the problem be from the user's / operator's / adversary's view?
- What problem would disappear if we changed the goal by 10%?

## 6. Why-why (non-threatening)

Ask "why?" until the reason is concrete enough to provoke against:

- Why do we have handles on coffee cups? → Because cups are too hot → provoke: `Po, coffee is not so hot in the first place` / `Po, cups have insulated grips, not handles`. *(de Bono's cup-handle example)*

## Output of this step

A short artifact you can hand to the provocation stage:

```
Dominant idea: We are optimizing _X_ by _Y_ because we assume _Z_.
Taken for granted: [2-3 bullets]
Boundary drawn at: [where the box is]
Real goal behind the stated problem: [one sentence]
Appetite for weirdness: low / medium / high  (+ time available)
```

Keep it to half a page. Your next step is to generate `Po:` statements with the 6 provocation operators.
