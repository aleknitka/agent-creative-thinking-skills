# Provenance — Attribution, Po Etymology, and Evidence

## What this skill is and is not

This skill implements **lateral thinking** as defined by **Edward de Bono** — a disciplined heuristic for interrupting dominant patterns (the "main track") and moving sideways to new frames, not a validated cognitive law.

- **Named method:** Edward de Bono — *The Use of Lateral Thinking* (1967), *Po: Beyond Yes and No* (1968; revised 1972), *Lateral Thinking: Creativity Step by Step* (1970), *Serious Creativity* (1992). Key examples used here (downstream factory, nose→photocopier, telephone once a year, coffee-cup handles, Nine Dots) are de Bono's and are used as illustration, not bundled text.
- **Adaptations:** The filing of tools into challenge / provocation + movement / random entry, the "6 operators × 5 movement moves" presentation, the intake template, harvest format (mechanism + assumption challenged + probe), and the mapping to other skills are practical workflow adaptations by this repository. They preserve de Bono's semantics but are not his wording. Call them adaptations when attributing.
- **No bundled third-party material:** Methods are summarized and linked; no copyrighted text is reproduced beyond short attributed facts.

## Po etymology

- **"Po"** was invented by de Bono as a **provocation operator** — a signal that the statement that follows is to be used for forward movement, not judgment. It is derived from ***hy-po-thesis*, *sup-po-se*, *po-ssible*, *po-etry*** — all words that indicate forward movement and share the syllable "po." De Bono describes it as a **provocative / provocation operation** marker.
- De Bono also notes a second resonance: in **Māori**, *po* refers to the original state of chaotic formlessness before evolution — a metaphor for the unstructured space a provocation opens.
- In this skill, every provocation is labelled `Po:` to keep the judgment/movement boundary visible.

## Evidence and limits

- Critics have characterized lateral thinking as **pseudo-scientific**; de Bono's core claims were not subjected to rigorous randomized controlled trials, and controlled studies of "de Bono training" show **mixed, small-to-moderate, facilitator-dependent** effects (see e.g., Mayer 1983 et al. on creativity training). This skill therefore presents the method as a **heuristic**, not a law.
- Do not cite the skill's provocations, analogies, or simulated perspectives as evidence. Tag claims as **fact / assumption / speculation**, and treat AI-generated examples as hypotheses to test — not findings.
- For a portable implementation, cite the method by name and edition when sharing the artifact externally; keep attribution in the artifact's header.
