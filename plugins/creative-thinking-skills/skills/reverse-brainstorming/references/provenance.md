# Provenance — Lineage, Attribution, and Evidence Limits

## The lineage

- **Alex Osborn (1888–1966)** codified brainstorming in *Applied Imagination* (1953): defer judgment, aim for quantity, welcome wild ideas, and combine or improve others' ideas. Reverse brainstorming inherits the first two rules directly — bad ideas are generated without judgment, in volume. The technique itself circulates in facilitation practice without a single canonical author or founding text; treat "reverse brainstorming" as a named variant of Osborn-style brainstorming rather than an invention with one source.
- **Bob Eberle** published the SCAMPER checklist in *SCAMPER: Games for Imagination Development* (1971), with "R" covering reverse and rearrange. Its "Reverse" prompt overlaps with this skill's move but applies to an existing product; reverse brainstorming instead inverts the mechanisms that sustain a problem. Attribute the checklist to Eberle, not the problem-worsening frame.
- **The inversion tradition.** Carl Jacobi's reported motto *"man muss immer umkehren"* ("one must always invert") and Charles Munger's popularization — "Invert, always invert," and the 1986 "Prescription for Misery" address — supply the reasoning habit of asking how to guarantee failure. Scott Adams popularized the practical prompt "How would I guarantee failure?" in *How to Fail at Almost Everything and Still Win Big* (2013). The Stoic practice of *premeditatio malorum* and the *via negativa* (Taleb, *Antifragile*, 2012) are adjacent traditions: they rehearse or define by the negative, but they aim at composure or robust knowledge, not at generating improvements.
- **Edward de Bono** included reversal among lateral-thinking techniques (*Lateral Thinking*, 1970): turn the situation around, then use the reversed version as a stepping stone. That is the closest methodological relative; de Bono's focus is escaping a pattern, while reverse brainstorming's focus is converting a deliberately bad idea into an improvement direction for a live problem.

## What is original here

These are workflow adaptations for this repository, not claims about the sources:

- The six-step process (intake, worsen, cluster by mechanism, reverse, enrich and de-risk, harvest).
- The four worsening directions (more frequent, more severe, more persistent, more contagious) and the domain sweep tuned to a persisting problem.
- The distinction between reversing clusters and reversing individual ideas, and the six reversal operators.
- The quality tests for bad ideas and for reversal directions.
- The separation rule (worsen and reverse are distinct passes with separate surfaces) and the 50-minute agenda.
- The language-learning worked example, comparison tables, and the offline `scripts/reverse.py` helper.

Attribute the core moves — deferred judgment and quantity (Osborn), the reverse prompt (Eberle / de Bono), and "ask how to guarantee failure" (Jacobi, Munger, Adams) — to those sources. Attribute the workflow above to this skill as an adaptation.

## Evidence and limits

- There is **no controlled study of reverse brainstorming as a named technique.** Its support is practitioner experience and adjacent research; do not present it as evidence-based in the clinical or policy sense.
- **Brainstorming groups underperform nominal groups.** Diehl and Stroebe (1987) found productivity loss in interacting groups relative to individuals generating alone, driven by production blocking and evaluation apprehension. This is the main reason this skill requires silent, independent generation before sharing; it does not validate the reversal step.
- **Considering the opposite can reduce bias.** Lord, Lepper, and Preston (1984) found that asking people to consider why an opposite conclusion might be true reduced assimilation bias. That supports the general move of arguing the other side; it does not show that worsening a problem produces better improvements.
- **Prospective hindsight.** Mitchell, Russo, and Pennington (1989), as cited in Klein's premortem work, reported roughly a 30% increase in reasons generated when people imagined an event had already happened. This concerns reason generation under a declared failure, and the figure should not be transferred to reverse brainstorming as an effect size.
- The method is qualitative. It surfaces plausible mechanisms and candidate directions; it does not estimate probabilities, discover unknown unknowns, or replace pilots, user research, or formal hazard analysis.
- Known biases: negativity and availability dominate the worsen pass, groups can converge on familiar complaints, and the first reversal is often the obvious baseline fix. Independent generation, the domain sweep, and the requirement for a mechanism are mitigations, not guarantees.
- A bad idea is **fiction** and a reversal is a **hypothesis**. Do not cite this skill's example, clusters, or concepts as evidence about your own problem.
- Attribution caution: the origin of "reverse brainstorming" is uncertain in the literature; phrase it as a widely used variant, and quote Munger's and Jacobi's ideas rather than contested verbatim lines.
